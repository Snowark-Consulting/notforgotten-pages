    Pattern Analysis

    James's standard follow-up sequence for client quotes:

    1. Initial outreach (3–7 days after quote sent by Travis): Introduction + "checking in"

    2. Second touch (7–14 days later): "Just wanted to touch base again"

    3. Third touch (7–21 days later): "Just wanted to circle back" — slightly more direct, mentions Travis's scheduling

    4. Final touch (14–30 days later): "Final Follow-Up" or closure

    Exception cases:
  • When a client replies, James responds within hours (same day)
  • Internal coordination with Sherie/Travis: rapid (< 1 day)
  • Long-cycle projects (Dragon Boat Shed, Board approvals): 30+ day intervals
  • The "Marlene Newton" thread shows the most intensive follow-up: 5 messages over ~2 months
    • Peak follow-up months: January–April 2026 (highest volume: 59, 58, 39, 41 messages respectively), corresponding to Travis Krieg Painting's quote pipeline being active.

    5. Response Patterns: What Got Replies vs. Ghosted

    Reply Rates by Approach

    Approach Messages Replies Observed Pattern
    "Introduction & your recent quote" 9 ~6 Highest reply rate — personalized, warm, name-drops Travis
    "Checking In" / "Just checking in" 64 ~15-20 Moderate reply rate — short, low-pressure
    "Following up on your painting quote" 8 ~4 Moderate — more direct but still gentle
    "Quick question about your quotes" (cold outreach) 40 ~8-10 ~20-25% reply rate to painters
    "Checking In" (cold outreach to painters) 43 ~5-8 ~12-18% reply rate — shorter template

    Characteristics of Messages That Got Replies

    1. Personalized with property/quote details — "your property in Vista", "the hallway painting works at your property in Edwardstown"

    2. Mentioned specific context — "I understand you were coordinating the timing around the new carpet and flooring"

    3. "No pressure at all" language — removes sales resistance

    4. Clear, single question — "how are you feeling about the proposal?" (not multiple questions)

    5. "Working with Travis Krieg Painting" signature — establishes legitimacy

    6. Moderate length — not too short, not too long (3-5 sentences of substance)

    Characteristics of Messages That Got Ghosted

    1. Generic "Checking In" subject — 43 sent with this subject, most to new prospects (untargeted)

    2. No recipient name — some templates opened with just "Hi,"

    3. Too sales-focused openers — "Quick question once you send a quote, do you usually follow it up, or does it go quiet?"

    4. Multiple touches with identical wording — the Jake Berryman case: 3 follow-ups, increasingly direct, all ignored

    7. Recommendations

    For James's Outreach Process

    1. Personalize the "Checking In" template for cold outreach

  • Current generic "Hi [Business Name]" approach has lowest reply rate
  • Add 1 sentence of specific research on each business (like the TPR Electrical email that got a reply)

    • 2. Add a "third path" between ghosted and converted

  • Current process: follow up 3 times → mark as lost
  • Consider adding a "long nurture" track: quarterly check-in for quotes that went cold but weren't explicitly declined
  • Dragon Boat Shed model (Steve Holt): "I shall keep you in the loop" → 33-day follow-up interval works for institutional clients

    • 3. A/B test quote expiry

  • 2-day expiry is extremely short and may cause clients to feel rushed
  • Consider testing 7-day expiry for residential quotes to see if conversion improves
  • The follow-up process needs the quote to still be "live" when James reaches out

    • 4. Capture "reason for loss" systematically

  • Currently: "lost as I haven't heard back from them" — no differentiation between ghost, went-with-competitor, timing, price
  • Adding a 1-question survey on close-out would build competitive intelligence

    • 5. Expand the "story-driven" template

  • "Quick question — how many quotes are slipping through?" (Template 3) uses the $120K recovery story — this is the most compelling cold outreach template
  • Consider making this the primary cold outreach email and A/B test against the "Quick Question" template

    • For the Travis Krieg Painting Relationship

    6. Reduce the handoff friction

  • When James says "I'll have Travis reach out to you," there's a delay while he forwards to Sherie, who then coordinates with Travis
  • Consider a shared calendar link for clients to book directly

    • 7. Track which quote options convert

  • Are Option 1 or Option 2 quotes more likely to convert?
  • Are multi-option quotes more likely to get a response than single-option?

    • 8. Shorten the quote-to-follow-up gap

  • James's introduction email goes out 3-7 days after the quote
  • With 2-day expiry, the quote is already expired when he reaches out
  • Move to same-day or next-day introduction
