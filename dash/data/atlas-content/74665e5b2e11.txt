# Learnings corruption, 13th occurrence — the writer hit three files, and one card lost a trap outright

**Confidence:** high
**Contested:** false

## What happened

The routine integrity guard (`scripts/bin/learnings-integrity-guard.py`) reported **38 findings**
against the working tree at the start of the 18 Sep consolidation — the largest count of any
occurrence (31 on the 12th, 30 on the 11th).

Three files, not the usual two:

| File | mtime (UTC) | ACST | Damage |
|---|---|---|---|
| `learnings/catalogue.md` | 2026-09-17 21:32:46.77 | 07:02:46 | warning block deleted, 2 rows dropped, 7 rows `\|\|`-prefixed, `campaign` cell linked, mid-table blank line |
| `learnings/cards/hermes.md` | 2026-09-17 21:32:47.87 | 07:02:47 | 2 `## TRAP` headings deleted, the `**UPDATE 2026-08-18:**` note deleted, one trap reworded |
| `learnings/cards/gmail.md` | 2026-09-17 21:56:23.97 | 07:26:23 | one trap deleted outright, another condensed, two traps reworded |

The first two are the familiar burst (1.1 s apart). The third is new: **`cards/gmail.md` was
rewritten ~24 minutes later, on its own.** No prior occurrence touched a third file, and no prior
occurrence ran two separate bursts in one night.

**New damage element:** `## TRAP — Customer-quoted date can disagree with the real send date;
verify before suspecting a duplicate send (2026-09-17)` was **deleted outright** from the gmail
card — heading and body. That trap was written on 17 Sep and was gone within a day. Nothing
downstream referenced it, so nothing else failed; the loss was only visible because the guard
compares heading lists against HEAD.

## The writer adds as well as deletes

The damage is not purely subtractive, which is what makes it hard to spot. This time the writer's
version of `cards/gmail.md` carried **one genuinely-new block** (the OAuth-refresh single-shot
trap from that morning's 07:20 gate failure) and, in `cards/hermes.md`, **one genuinely-new block**
(the `write_file` append-only-log trap from 17 Sep). Both were real learnings of the day; both
appear only in the writer's damaged version.

So the writer behaves like a stale, lossy merge of the day's learnings into the cards, not a
random corruption.

## Repair applied

1. `git restore learnings/catalogue.md learnings/cards/hermes.md learnings/cards/gmail.md`
   → guard immediately silent PASS. HEAD is the clean reference because the 21:30 backup commits
   before the ~21:33+ write.
2. **Duplicate check before re-applying** (the 12th-event rule). Both genuinely-new blocks were
   checked against the card layer and re-applied, because neither existed in any card:
   - the OAuth-refresh trap → appended to `cards/gmail.md`;
   - the `write_file` trap → appended to `cards/hermes.md`.
   The writer's *reworded copies of existing traps* were deliberately NOT re-applied.
3. One genuine content correction restored: the gmail card's `STILL SINGLE-SHOT: inbox-watchdog-gate.sh`
   sentence, which the 12th-event card carried, was superseded by that morning's hardening of all
   three gates. Replaced with the RESOLVED 2026-09-18 statement rather than left stale.
4. Catalogue warning block updated for the 13th event via deterministic exact-string replacement
   (never `patch`); `write_file` + `append-only log` added to the `hermes` routing keywords.
5. Post-repair verification: guard silent PASS; 23 table lines (header + separator + 21 rows);
   0 `||` rows; 21 cards on disk ↔ 21 rows; heading LIST diff vs HEAD shows **additions only**,
   zero deletions.

Nothing was weakened to make tonight pass: the guard is untouched, its always-zero exit was not
"fixed", and the backup pre-commit gate stays in place.

## Lessons

- **A clean guard result on HEAD does not mean the tree is clean.** The guard runs 21:20 and the
  backup 21:30; the write lands ~21:33+. Run the guard manually as the LAST step of every
  consolidation — it cannot see your own edits otherwise, and it cannot see the writer's.
- **Count the files, not just the findings.** The signature had held at exactly two files for
  five nights; this one hit three, with a second burst 24 minutes later. Treat "which files"
  as a variable, never a constant.
- **A deleted trap is silent in isolation.** `cards/gmail.md` lost a trap written the previous
  day and nothing broke. Only the HEAD heading-list diff surfaced it. `git diff --numstat` on
  any modified card, read before any repair, is the cheapest possible detector.
- **The writer is a lossy merge, so its added blocks are real.** Restoring from HEAD throws away
  genuine learnings unless you extract and re-apply them — which is why the repair is
  "restore, then extract, then duplicate-check, then re-apply", in that order.
