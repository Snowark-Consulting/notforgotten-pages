# Website Copy — Proposed Refinements

**Date:** 6 August 2026
**Status:** Awaiting James approval
**Standard:** Refinement, not reinvention. Same architecture. Sharper words.
**Key decisions applied:** Health Check removed (James directive). No painting-specific language. No email-only framing.

---

## 1. Page Title (Browser Tab / Search Results)

**Current:** Not Forgotten Systems | Automated Quote Follow-Up for Your Business

**Proposed:** Not Forgotten Systems | Never Lose A Quote To Silence

**Why stronger:** "Automated Quote Follow-Up" describes a feature. "Never Lose A Quote To Silence" describes the outcome — and creates curiosity. The word "silence" is more evocative than "gone cold" or "lost." It implies the customer didn't reject the quote; they just stopped communicating.

**Belief created:** There's a difference between losing a job and losing touch.

**Verification needed:** None.

**Confidence:** 85%

**Recommendation:** Replace.

## 2. Hero

**Current:**
> Never lose a job because a quote went cold.
>
> CC us on your quotes. We follow up by email and SMS until your customer makes a decision. You focus on the work. The follow-up takes care of itself.

**Alternatives considered (headline):**

| # | Headline | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Some of your best opportunities haven't said no. They've just gone quiet. | Reframes silence as uncertainty, not rejection. "Best opportunities" raises the stakes. "Gone quiet" is visual and relatable. | Two sentences. Slightly longer. |
| B | Silence isn't a no. | Short. Punchy. Creates curiosity. | Doesn't explain what it means quickly enough for first-time visitors. |
| C | They didn't say no. They just stopped replying. | Direct. Relatable. Names the exact experience. | Could feel accusatory toward the customer. |
| D | Some of your quotes aren't dead. They're just waiting. | Short. "Dead" is provocative. "Waiting" is active. | "Waiting" is a bit passive — waiting for what? |

**Proposed (headline):**
> Some of your best opportunities haven't said no. They've just gone quiet.

**Proposed (subhead):**
> We follow up every quote you send — by email, by SMS — until your customer makes a decision. You focus on the work. We keep the conversation alive.

**Why stronger:** "Best opportunities" frames the problem as valuable, not just urgent. "Haven't said no" is precise — it's what's true. The customer hasn't rejected anything. "Gone quiet" is a phrase business owners already use. The subhead shifts from "the follow-up takes care of itself" (passive, mechanical) to "we keep the conversation alive" (active, human, relationship-focused).

**Belief created:** The quotes that went quiet might still be winnable — and they're among the best ones we sent.

**Verification needed:** None — this is positioning, not a factual claim.

**Confidence:** 85%

**Recommendation:** Replace. Version A is the strongest — it reframes silence as uncertainty, raises the stakes with "best opportunities," and uses language the visitor already thinks in.

**Current subhead:** Adjust the sliders to match your business. See how much revenue Not Forgotten could recover from quotes that would otherwise go cold.

**Proposed headline:** See What You're Leaving On The Table *(keep)*

**Proposed subhead:** Enter your numbers below. See how much could be sitting in quotes that never got a second look. Based on what we've recovered for businesses like yours. No signup. No pitch.

"Based on what we've recovered for businesses like yours" adds credibility without making a specific promise. "No signup. No pitch." removes friction — this is a discovery tool, not a funnel.

**Belief created:** I can see my own potential, right now, without committing to anything.

**Verification needed:** "Businesses like yours" — we have QPaint and Travis Krieg data. Acceptable as directional, not absolute.

**Confidence:** 85%

**Recommendation:** Refine subhead. Keep headline and calculator as is.

## 5. How It Works

**Current:**
> Never Wonder What Happened To A Quote Again
>
> Step 1: Send Your Quote — Send your quote exactly as you do today. Simply CC or BCC Not Forgotten into the email and our system automatically tracks the opportunity. No new tools. No new process. No extra steps.
>
> Step 2: Automated Follow-Up Begins — Not Forgotten follows up by email and SMS at the right time to keep the conversation moving and ensure your quote doesn't go cold. No manual chasing. No additional admin. No extra work for your team.
>
> Step 3: Customer Responds & You Get Notified — When your customer responds, Not Forgotten tracks the outcome and keeps you informed. Whether they accept the quote, ask a question, or need more time, you will always know where every opportunity stands.
>
> Step 4: Monthly Insights Show You The Patterns — Because every follow-up creates data, we send you a one-page report each month. Close rates by job type. Revenue trends. Which quotes went cold. No dashboard. No login. Just patterns worth noticing.

**Proposed:**

> **How It Works**
>
> **1. You send the quote.**
> Business as usual. CC us in. Nothing changes about how you work.
>
> **2. We pick up the conversation.**
> Your customer hears from us by email and SMS. Not pushy. Not salesy. Just a conversation that keeps moving. You're on the tools. We're managing the relationship.
>
> **3. They reply. You know.**
> The moment a customer responds, you're told. They want to proceed. They have a question. They need more time. Whatever it is, you always know where things stand.
>
> **4. Patterns start showing up.**
> Each month you get a single page showing what you'd never spot on your own. Which quotes close. Which go quiet. What might be worth a phone call. No dashboard. No login. Coffee-readable.

**Why stronger:** Each step is one sentence the visitor can scan in a second. The language is active ("you send", "we pick up", "they reply") rather than passive system descriptions ("Automated Follow-Up Begins"). "You're on the tools" speaks directly to trades — they know exactly what this means. "Patterns start showing up" makes the report feel like an organic outcome, not a bolt-on feature.

**Belief created:** This doesn't change how I work. It just adds a layer I didn't have before.

**Verification needed:** None — these are process descriptions.

**Confidence:** 80%

**Recommendation:** Replace all four steps.

## Rhythm and Emotional Journey

| Moment | What the visitor feels |
|---|---|
| **Hero** | Recognition. "Some of my best opportunities *have* gone quiet." |
| **Social proof** | Credibility. "This actually works. Fast." |
| **ROI Calculator** | Discovery. "Let me see my own numbers." |
| **How It Works** | Relief. "This doesn't change how I work." |
| **Report mockup** | Curiosity. "I'd want to see that for my business." |
| **Travis/Results** | Trust. "This happened to someone real." |
| **Founder story** | Connection. "This person gets it." |
| **Pricing** | Decision. "The maths works." |
| **Industries** | Belonging. "This is for businesses like mine." |
| **Footer** | The question. "How many of mine have gone quiet?" |

**Through-line:** "Gone quiet" appears in the hero, the results section, the founder story, and the footer. It's the unifying idea. Every section either creates curiosity about what's been lost to silence or provides evidence that it can be recovered. No word is wasted. No feature is listed. Every sentence earns its place.
