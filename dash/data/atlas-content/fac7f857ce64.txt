# Website Positioning Review — Version 2

**Date:** 5 August 2026
**Author:** Atlas
**Status:** ⏳ Pending James Approval
**Type:** Positioning Architecture (not copy review)
**Principle:** Recognition before explanation. Problem before product.

> This is not a copy review. This defines what the website must make the visitor feel, recognise, and believe — before a single word of copy is written. The message must be stronger than the software.

---

## 1. The Core Insight

### What We Know

Customers rarely say "no." Life gets busy. Insurance takes time. Committees delay. Emails get buried. People get distracted.

Businesses interpret silence as rejection. They shouldn't. Silence usually means: nobody followed up consistently enough for the customer to make a decision at all.

We proved this today. A $129,750 quote sat dormant for 365 days. The customer had already approved it — the body corporate committee had voted. Nobody had asked. One email. Four hours. Done.

The money was already there. The business just didn't know.

### The Positioning This Creates

Not Forgotten does not exist because follow-up is hard.

Not Forgotten exists because **businesses are leaving money on the table and they don't know it.**

That is the problem the website must make visitors recognise.

Not "you need better follow-up." Not "automation saves time." Not "AI is cool."

"You have revenue sitting inside your business right now that you don't know about. We find it."

## 2. The Recognition Framework

### The visitor should think these thoughts — in this order

| Order | The Thought | The Section |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "That happens in my business." | Hero |
| 2 | "I wonder how much I've lost." | Problem |
| 3 | "There's a reason this keeps happening." | Insight |
| 4 | "Someone else fixed this." | Proof |
| 5 | "This could work for me too." | How |
| 6 | "The economics make sense." | Value |
| 7 | "I want to know what I'm leaving on the table." | Action |

Every section must trigger the next thought. The visitor should never pause to think about Not Forgotten — they should be thinking about their own business.

### The Wrong Order (Current Site)

| Order | Current Site Says | Visitor Thinks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Never lose a job because a quote went cold." | "OK, another follow-up tool." |
| 2 | "CC us on your quotes. We follow up by email and SMS." | "You want access to my email?" |
| 3 | "$177K recovered." | "Maybe. But that's one business." |
| 4 | "Here's how it works." | "I've seen this before." |

The current site asks the visitor to understand Not Forgotten before they've recognised their own problem. That's backwards.

## 3. The "So What?" Ladder

### Every claim must survive this chain

**Starting point:** "We automate quote follow-up."

| Layer | So What? | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | So what? | "Fewer opportunities fall through the cracks." |
| 2 | So what? | "You recover revenue that would have been lost." |
| 3 | So what? | "You have greater confidence in your pipeline." |
| 4 | So what? | "You can make better business decisions." |
| 5 | So what? | "Your business grows without you doing more work." |

**Layer 5 is the real outcome.** Layer 1 is a feature. The current website stops at Layer 2 ("recover revenue"). The positioning needs to reach Layer 4-5 — confidence, decisions, growth — because that's what business owners actually want.

### Another chain — starting from the problem

**Starting point:** "Quotes go cold because nobody follows up."

| Layer | So What? | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | So what? | "Customers assume you're not interested." |
| 2 | So what? | "They go elsewhere — or nowhere at all." |
| 3 | So what? | "You lose revenue you never knew was available." |
| 4 | So what? | "You make business decisions based on incomplete information." |
| 5 | So what? | "You build a business that's smaller than it should be." |

**Layer 5 is the emotional trigger.** "Smaller than it should be." That's what a business owner feels at 2am. Not "I need better follow-up software." The website must reach that feeling.

## 4. Section-by-Section Positioning Intent

*This defines what each section must achieve. It does not prescribe copy — only the emotional and intellectual job the section must perform.*

### Hero Section

**Job:** Make the visitor recognise themselves.

**The thought we want:** "That happens in my business. I've wondered about those quotes."

**The feeling we want:** Unease. Curiosity. A slight sting of recognition.

**What we are NOT doing:**
- Explaining what Not Forgotten does
- Mentioning AI, automation, or technology
- Making a promise about our product
- Leading with a number

**What we ARE doing:**
- Describing a universal business experience: sending a quote, hearing nothing, assuming it's dead
- Naming the silent assumption: "they probably went elsewhere"
- Planting doubt: "but what if they didn't?"

**Positioning concept:** The money is already in your business. You just haven't found it yet.

### The Insight Section (new — between hero and how-it-works)

**Job:** Explain why good businesses lose work. Not what NF does about it — why it happens at all.

**The thought we want:** "I've never thought about it that way. That explains a lot."

**What we are NOT doing:**
- Introducing the product
- Talking about our process
- Selling anything

**What we ARE doing:**
- Naming the real reasons quotes go cold: busy customers, insurance delays, committee timelines, buried emails
- Revealing the false assumption: silence = rejection
- Introducing the real pattern: customers rarely say no. They just never get around to saying yes.

**Positioning concept:** You haven't been rejected. You've been forgotten. There's a difference.

### Proof / Evidence Section

**Job:** Show that the problem is solvable. Not that NF is impressive — that other businesses have recovered money they thought was lost.

**The thought we want:** "Someone else had this exact problem and it got fixed."

**What we are NOT doing:**
- Using QPaint stats publicly (reserved until Tas approval)
- Listing NF capabilities
- Making theoretical arguments

**What we ARE doing:**
- Showing Travis Krieg: $177K recovered, real business, real quotes, real money
- Framing it as: "this business had the same problem you have"
- Making the connection: "these were quotes the owner thought were dead"

**Positioning concept:** This isn't a promise. It's evidence. Other businesses already recovered this money. Yours is still sitting there.

### How It Works

**Job:** Show that the solution is simple. Not impressive — simple. The visitor should think "that's all it takes?"

**The thought we want:** "I could do that. Actually, I should have been doing that."

**What we are NOT doing:**
- Describing our technology
- Listing features (email sequences, SMS, tracking, insights)
- Explaining AI or automation
- Making it sound complex or clever

**What we ARE doing:**
- Showing that the process is almost embarrassingly simple
- Positioning it as: "you send quotes the same way you always do. Everything else happens without you."
- Making the monthly insights report feel like discovery, not data: "you learn things about your own business you never noticed"

**Positioning concept:** The solution isn't complicated. The problem was that nobody was doing it.

### The Monthly Insights Report

**Job:** Show the visitor that their business has patterns they've never seen.

**The thought we want:** "I don't know those numbers about my own business. I should."

**What we are NOT doing:**
- Positioning this as a "bonus feature"
- Using the word "AI" or "analytics"
- Making it feel like a dashboard

**What we ARE doing:**
- Showing a real-looking report (the current mockup is good)
- Framing it as: "this is what you learn when someone actually pays attention to your quotes"
- Making the questions in the report feel provocative: "Is this a follow-up issue, or something about how these quotes are priced?"

**Positioning concept:** Most business owners know less about their own quoting patterns than they think. The report is a mirror — sometimes uncomfortable, always useful.

### Value / Pricing

**Job:** Make the economics undeniable. The visitor should do the maths themselves and conclude it's irrational not to try.

**The thought we want:** "At that price, I'd only need to recover one job a year for this to pay for itself."

**What we are NOT doing:**
- Defending the price ("no tiers, no upsells")
- Listing features as justification for cost
- Comparing to competitors

**What we ARE doing:**
- Simple number: $199/month. That's it.
- Reframing: "One recovered quote covers years of this."
- Making the setup fee feel like the only friction — and a small one

**Positioning concept:** The price is not the decision. The decision is: do you want to know what you're leaving on the table?

### Call to Action

**Job:** Convert recognition into action. The visitor has seen the problem, seen the proof, seen the simplicity. Now they need a low-friction way to find out what's sitting in their own business.

**The thought we want:** "I want to know what my quotes are worth."

**What we are NOT doing:**
- "Start Quote Follow-up" — asks them to commit to using a tool
- "Book a Demo" — feels like a sales call
- "Get a Free Health Check" — already flagged for removal

**What we ARE doing:**
- Inviting discovery, not commitment
- Making the next step feel obvious, not risky
- "See What Your Quotes Are Worth" or equivalent — outcome-led, curiosity-driven

**Positioning concept:** You don't have to buy anything. You just have to be curious enough to find out.

## 5. The Positioning Architecture

### The Narrative Arc

```
1. HERO — "You have quotes out there. You think they're dead. They probably aren't."
   
2. INSIGHT — "Here's why quotes actually go cold. It's not rejection. It's silence."
   
3. PROOF — "Other businesses recovered money they thought was lost. Travis Krieg: $177K."
   
4. HOW — "The solution is simple. You send quotes the same way. Everything else happens."
   
5. INSIGHTS — "You learn things about your business you never noticed. Every month."
   
6. VALUE — "$199/month. One recovered job covers years of this."
   
7. ACTION — "See what your quotes are worth."
```

### What We Never Say

| Don't Say | Because |
|---|---|
| "Automated" | Sounds like spam. The product is human-sounding follow-up. |
| "AI-powered" | Customers don't buy AI. They buy outcomes. |
| "Never lose a job" | Defensive framing. Reframe to "money you didn't know was there." |
| "Follow-up sequences" | Feature language. The customer doesn't care about sequences. |
| "Track responses" | Creepy. Reframe to "you learn what your customers are thinking." |
| "Dashboard" | Exhausting. Business owners don't want another dashboard. |
| "Platform" | Enterprise language. NF is a service, not a platform. |
| "Scale" | Investor language. The customer wants their problem solved, not "scale." |
| "Free" anything | Positions NF as something you try because it's free, not because it's valuable. |

## 6. Competitive Moats That Stay Invisible

These are what make NF different. They improve customer outcomes. They are NOT what customers buy. They stay off the website.

| Capability | Why It Matters | Why It Stays Hidden |
|---|---|---|
| Production verification | Ensures emails actually deliver | Customer never needs to know. They just need the outcome. |
| Reconciliation audits | CRM always reflects reality | Internal discipline. Invisible to customer. |
| BK principles | Systematic learning from every reply | Competitive advantage. Don't teach competitors. |
| Evidence dossiers | Institutional-grade record keeping | Client-facing deliverable, not public marketing. |
| Multi-client architecture | Designed for 100 clients | Internal. Customers care about their experience, not our architecture. |
| Incident reviews | Continuous improvement loop | Internal. Turning failures into strength is a process, not a promise. |

## 7. What Changes vs What Stays

### Remove

- Health Check section (James-directed)
- "Start Quote Follow-up" CTA
- "Get a Free Quote Health Check" CTA
- "Automated" language throughout
- Feature lists (email sequences, SMS, tracking — reframe as outcomes)

### Add

- Insight section explaining why quotes really go cold
- Recognition language: "That happens in my business"
- Outcome-led CTA: discovery, not commitment
- Trust reassurance: "We never contact your customers without you"

### Reframe

- How It Works → from process to simplicity
- Pricing → from features to undeniable economics
- Monthly insights → from "bonus" to core value
- Hero → from feature promise to problem recognition

### Preserve

- ROI Calculator (works — keep the concept, adjust framing)
- Travis Krieg case study (strong, real, approved)
- Founder story (authentic, personal, credible)
- Simple pricing ($199/month, $99 setup)
- Monthly insights mockup (excellent visual evidence)

## 9. The Test

After positioning is approved and copy is rewritten, the website must pass this test:

**A business owner reads the homepage. Before reaching the CTA, they think at least three of these thoughts:**

- [ ] "That's exactly what happens in my business."
- [ ] "I've definitely lost money this way."
- [ ] "I don't actually know which quotes are still alive."
- [ ] "I assumed those were dead. Maybe they weren't."
- [ ] "One recovered job would pay for a year of this."
- [ ] "I want to know what's sitting in my own quote history."

If they think zero or one of these, the positioning has failed. The copy doesn't matter yet — fix the positioning first.

If they think four or more, the positioning is working. Then — and only then — write the copy.
