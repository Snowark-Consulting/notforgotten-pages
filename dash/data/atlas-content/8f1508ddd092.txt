# Experiment: Travis-Led Email 1 — Painter Pilot

**Status:** Awaiting James approval
**Date:** 2 August 2026
**Review:** After all 19 prospects complete the 4-touch sequence

---

## Experiment Design

### Hypothesis

Leading with credible social proof from a business like theirs (Travis Krieg Painting: $177,555 recovered, 14 jobs, 4 months) will increase engagement compared to curiosity-led Email 1s.

### What We're Testing

Not "should Travis be our permanent Email 1." We're testing whether social proof at first touch generates more meaningful replies than the industry-observation approach used in July (0/65 Email 1 replies).

### The Evidence That Led Here

| Touchpoint | Emails | Meaningful Replies | Conversions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email 1 (curiosity/observation) | ~65 | 0 | 0 |
| Email 2 (process questions) | ~60 | 0 | 0 |
| **Email 3 (Travis case study)** | **~60** | **2** | **2** |
| Email 4 (breakup) | 60 | — | — |

Travis converted at Email 3 in both cases where prospects reached it. Nothing converted before it. The question is whether the case study needs the first two emails to build context, or whether it works as an opener.

---

## Success Criteria

### What Success Looks Like

| Metric | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reply rate | ≥5% (1+ replies from 19) | July Email 1: 0/65 = 0%. Any reply is improvement |
| Meaningful reply rate | ≥1 T3+ reply | T3 = engaged. T1-2 = polite dismissal |
| Time to first reply | ≤5 days | July Email 3 replies came within 48h |
| Meetings booked | ≥1 | The conversion metric |

### What Would Prove This Wrong

If **0/19 prospects generate a meaningful reply (T3+)**, the hypothesis is challenged. We would not conclude "Travis doesn't work" — we know it converts at Email 3. We would conclude "Travis at Email 1 without prior context does not outperform curiosity-led Email 1s."

In that scenario, the recommendation would be: retire Travis-led Email 1 and return to a two-touch build-up (curiosity → Travis).

### What Success Would Mean

If the pilot generates ≥1 meaningful reply within 5 days, we expand to a second wave of 20-25 painters using the same sequence. After two waves with consistent positive signal, we consider adopting it as the standard painter Email 1.

## The 4-Email Sequence

### Email 1 (Day 0) — Travis-Led Opening

**Subject:** A painter in Adelaide recovered $177K

**Purpose:** Introduce credible social proof. Make them think "that could be me." Earn a reply.

**Body:**

> Hi [Name or Business],
>
> I run a service that helps painters follow up on quotes they've sent. Before I explain how it works, I wanted to share something that happened recently.
>
> A painter in Adelaide, Travis Krieg, CC'd us on his quotes over four months. 14 jobs came back from clients who'd gone completely silent. The total recovered was $177,555.
>
> He was running the business, quoting jobs, managing a crew. The follow-up was happening when he remembered. We made sure nothing slipped through.
>
> Curious — is this something you've experienced? Quotes that go quiet and never come back?

**Design notes:**
- Leads with the story, not the company
- "$177,555" — exact number, not rounded. Builds credibility
- "14 jobs" — specific volume, shows it wasn't one lucky break
- "When he remembered" — assumes competence, names the friction
- One question. Easy to reply to
- No product pitch. No CTA beyond the question
- 97 words. ~12 seconds to read

---

### Email 2 (Day +5) — Different Angle

**Subject:** The interesting part wasn't the number

**Purpose:** Add a new observation. Show we thought about this more. Don't repeat Email 1.

**Body:**

> Hi [Name or Business],
>
> I reached out last week about a painter who recovered $177K from silent quotes.
>
> The interesting part to me wasn't the dollar figure. It was that most of those 14 jobs came back in the first three weeks. After that, the recovery rate dropped sharply.
>
> It made me wonder how many quotes are sitting in inboxes right now — not dead, just waiting for someone to follow them up. The window is real, and it's shorter than most people think.
>
> Do you have a sense of how long your quotes typically sit before someone follows them up? Or does it depend on who sent it?

**Design notes:**
- References Email 1 but doesn't assume engagement
- "I reached out" not "as I mentioned" — honest about unilateral contact
- New insight: the time window. Adds value for anyone who read both emails
- Question is practical and easy to answer
- 105 words

---

### Email 3 (Day +8) — Process Question

**Subject:** How do you handle silent quotes?

**Purpose:** Make them reflect on their own process. Bridge to the problem we solve.

**Body:**

> Hi [Name or Business],
>
> I've been thinking about how different painting businesses handle the gap between sending a quote and getting an answer.
>
> Some owners I've spoken to have a system — they block out Friday afternoons for follow-up calls. Others rely on whoever sent the quote remembering to chase it. A few have told me they just move on and hope the phone rings.
>
> No wrong answers here. I'm genuinely curious — where does your business sit on that spectrum?
>
> James

**Design notes:**
- No product mention. Pure curiosity
- "Some owners I've spoken to" — social proof without the case study
- Three options presented without judgment — lets them self-identify
- "No wrong answers" — reduces pressure to give the "right" response
- 80 words. Quick read

---

### Email 4 (Day +15) — Breakup

**Subject:** last one from me

**Purpose:** Close the sequence respectfully. Leave the door open. Protect the relationship.

**Body:**

> Hi [Name or Business],
>
> No worries if the timing isn't right.
>
> If anything changes down the track, just reply to this email.
>
> All the best with the work ahead.

**Design notes:**
- Standard breakup template. 26 words, 10 seconds to read
- No new persuasion. No repeated case study
- Clean, professional close
- Leaves a simple path for future contact

2. Classify all replies using Reply Quality Taxonomy (T0-T5)

6. Recommend: Adopt / Refine / Retire

## What Must Be True For This To Be Wrong

If 0/19 prospects generate a T3+ reply across the entire 4-touch sequence, the hypothesis that "leading with social proof at Email 1 increases engagement" is challenged. We return to the July structure (curiosity → process → Travis → breakup) and test other Email 1 variants.

A null result on Email 1 alone (0 replies) does not kill the hypothesis if Email 2 or 3 generates replies — that would suggest the Travis story still converts, just not as a cold opener.
