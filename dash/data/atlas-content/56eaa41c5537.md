## Failure Structure

Every failure entry follows this format:

1. **The email** — what we sent
2. **What we expected** — the outcome we predicted
3. **What actually happened** — the real outcome (no reply, negative reply, spam complaint, etc.)
4. **What went wrong** — our analysis of why it failed
5. **Which principle did we violate?** — or was the principle correct but the execution wrong?
6. **What we changed** — the specific fix to the philosophy, skill, or process
7. **Prevention** — how we'll avoid this failure in future

---

## Failure Categories

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| **Wrong fit** | The business didn't have the problem we assumed. Our research was wrong. |
| **Wrong question** | The question didn't resonate. Too generic, too assumptive, or irrelevant. |
| **Wrong timing** | The email was fine but the prospect wasn't in a position to engage. |
| **Execution failure** | The principle was right, the email was wrong. Poor personalisation, too long, too salesy. |
| **Principle violation** | We broke a principle and it cost us. The philosophy was right; we ignored it. |
| **Unknown** | The failure has no clear cause yet. Track and revisit. |

---

## The Failure Rule

**Every failure must produce a change.** If an email fails and nothing changes — no process updated, no principle reinforced, no skill improved — the failure was wasted. The goal isn't to avoid failure. It's to never fail the same way twice.
