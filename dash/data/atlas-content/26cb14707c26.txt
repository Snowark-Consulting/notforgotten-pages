# Website Copy Audit — 6 August 2026

**Assignment:** Section-by-section critique with wording improvements only. No redesign. No layout changes.

---

## Section 1: Hero

**Current:**
> Never lose a job because a quote went cold.
> CC us on your quotes. We follow up by email and SMS until your customer makes a decision. You focus on the work. The follow-up takes care of itself.

**Critique:**
Describes what we do. Doesn't make anyone feel anything. "Never lose a job" is a negative promise — prevention, not discovery. "CC us on your quotes" is a feature instruction. The visitor hasn't even recognised they have a problem yet and we're already telling them how to use the product.

The visitor's mental state when they land: "What is this?" They don't yet know they have quotes going cold. They don't track it. They don't think about it. The headline should create the recognition that something invisible is happening inside their business, not promise to prevent a loss they haven't acknowledged.

**Recommendation:**

> **Headline:** Some of your quotes aren't dead. They're just waiting.
>
> **Subhead:** We follow up with every quote you send — by email and SMS — until your customer makes a decision. You do the work. We make sure nothing slips through.
>
> **Confidence:** 85%
>
> **Psychological impact:** "Some of your quotes aren't dead" creates immediate curiosity. Every business owner has sent quotes that went quiet. They assumed silence meant no. This reframes it: silence might just mean they haven't decided yet. It plants a question they can't un-see when they next look at their sent folder.

**What stays:** The structure (headline + subhead + CTA placement). The pricing line below.

## Section 2: Social Proof Bar

**Current:** $177K+ Recovered by One Business | $59K Single Client in 3 Weeks | 14 Jobs Recovered Jan-Apr 2026

**Critique:**
Strong numbers. Good placement. The metrics tell a story. But "14 Jobs Recovered" is the weakest of the three — it's a count, not a value. The $59K stat is powerful but the label "Single Client in 3 Weeks" doesn't explain what happened.

**Recommendation:**

> **$177,555** Recovered from one business in 4 months
> **$59,000** 3 weeks. One client. Jobs from months earlier.
> **14 jobs** That would have been lost without follow-up

**Confidence:** 90%

**Psychological impact:** The middle stat now tells a micro-story — speed + value + surprise. "Jobs from months earlier" reinforces the discovery theme: there's money sitting in old quotes. The third stat reframes "recovered" as "would have been lost" — creates the "what if" question.

## Section 3: ROI Calculator

>

**What stays:** The calculator itself. The headline. The annual/monthly/ROI display. The disclaimer about Travis Krieg.

## Section 4: How It Works

**Current:**
> Never Wonder What Happened To A Quote Again
> 
> Step 1: Send Your Quote — Send your quote exactly as you do today. Simply CC or BCC Not Forgotten...
> Step 2: Automated Follow-Up Begins — Not Forgotten follows up by email and SMS at the right time...
> Step 3: Customer Responds & You Get Notified — When your customer responds...
> Step 4: Monthly Insights Show You The Patterns — Because every follow-up creates data...

**Critique:**
"Never Wonder What Happened To A Quote Again" is about not wondering — negative, reactive. The steps describe a process but don't create an emotional response. "Automated Follow-Up Begins" sounds like software. "Customer Responds & You Get Notified" is a notification — not a benefit.

The deeper problem: this section explains what we do. It doesn't help the visitor recognise what's happening in their own business. James's insight from the Carlie Gunn conversation applies here too — we shouldn't just describe follow-up. We should describe the *discovery*.

**Recommendation:**

> **Headline:** What Happens After You Send A Quote
>
> **Step 1 — You send the quote.** Business as usual. CC us in. Nothing changes about your process.
>
> **Step 2 — We pick up the conversation.** Your customer receives follow-ups by email and SMS. Not pushy. Not salesy. Just a conversation that keeps moving forward. You're back on the tools. We're managing the relationship.
>
> **Step 3 — They reply. You know.** The moment a customer responds, you're notified. They want to proceed. They have a question. They need more time. Whatever it is, you know where every opportunity stands.
>
> **Step 4 — Patterns start to emerge.** A monthly report shows you things you didn't see before. Which quotes close fastest. Which go quiet. What might be worth a phone call. No dashboard. No login. Coffee-readable.

**Confidence:** 80%

**Psychological impact:** "What Happens After You Send A Quote" reframes the section — it's about the customer's experience, not our process. "We pick up the conversation" sounds human, not automated. "Back on the tools" speaks directly to trades. The pattern language in Step 4 connects to the ROI Calculator's discovery moment — the system doesn't just follow up, it reveals things you didn't know.

## Section 5: Monthly Insights Report (Sample)

**Current:**
> You also get a monthly insights report
> Because every follow-up creates data, we send you a one-page report each month. Patterns you didn't see. Questions worth asking. Coffee-readable. No login required.

**Critique:**
The concept is excellent — the report is a differentiator. But the description undersells it. "Patterns you didn't see" is good. "Questions worth asking" is good. But the framing makes it sound like a nice extra, not a core reason to use the product.

**Recommendation:**

> **Headline:** Your Business, In One Page. Every Month.
>
> **Subhead:** Every follow-up creates data. Every reply teaches us something. At the end of each month, you get a single page that shows you what you'd never spot on your own. No dashboard. No login. No extra work. Just insight you can act on.
>
> **Confidence:** 80%
>
> **Psychological impact:** "Your business, in one page" makes it feel like a valuable asset, not a bonus feature. "What you'd never spot on your own" reinforces the discovery theme. The sample report mockup does the heavy lifting visually — the words just need to frame it as central to the product, not peripheral.

**What stays:** The sample report mockup. It's excellent.

## Section 6: "Not Ready To Commit?" / Quote Health Check

**Current:**
> Not Ready To Commit?
> Get a free Quote Health Check
> Send us some of your recent quote emails. We'll send you back a one-page report showing patterns you might not have noticed. No pitch. No obligation. Just your numbers, clearly presented.

**Critique:**
"Not Ready To Commit?" works against us. It tells the visitor they shouldn't commit — we're planting the objection ourselves. "Quote Health Check" sounds like a free consultation offer, which feels salesy even when we say it isn't. The offer itself is strong — a free report with no strings — but the framing weakens it.

**Recommendation:**

> **Headline:** See Your Own Numbers First
>
> **Subhead:** Forward a few recent quote emails to quotes@notforgotten.com.au. We'll send you back a page showing what we find. No pitch. No obligation. If nothing else, you'll see your quoting data in a way you probably haven't before.
>
> CTA: Send Your Quotes → *(remains the same)*
>
> **Confidence:** 85%
>
> **Psychological impact:** "See Your Own Numbers First" is about discovery, not hesitation. It assumes the visitor is interested — they just want evidence before committing. "If nothing else, you'll see your quoting data in a way you probably haven't before" is honest and low-pressure — it acknowledges they might not buy, but they'll still get value.

This puts the visitor INSIDE Travis's experience. They're not reading a testimonial. They're overhearing someone who just discovered the same thing they might be about to discover.

**What stays:** The Travis testimonial text. The founder photo and story. The case study link.

## Section 8: Pricing

**Current:**
> No Tiers. No Upsells. One Plan.
> Simple, Transparent Pricing
> $199/month | $99 One-Time Setup Fee
> [Feature list]
> How The Setup Fee Works

**Critique:**
"No Tiers. No Upsells. One Plan." is confident and clear. The pricing itself is straightforward. But the feature list ("Automated quote follow-up", "Email follow-up sequences", "Customer response tracking") is a list of things the visitor doesn't yet value. They don't know they need "email follow-up sequences." They know they need to stop losing work to silence.

**Recommendation:**

> **Headline:** One Plan. Everything Included.
>
> **Features:**
> - We follow up every quote you send
> - Email and SMS, for as long as it takes
> - You know the moment a customer replies
> - Monthly report showing what you'd never spot on your own
> - One price. No surprises. Cancel anytime.
>
> **Confidence:** 75%
>
> **Psychological impact:** The feature list now describes what happens, not what the software has. "We follow up every quote you send" — active, human. "For as long as it takes" — commitment, not a feature limit. "Showing what you'd never spot on your own" — ties back to the discovery theme. The features are the same, but the framing has shifted from product specs to outcomes.

**What stays:** The price. The setup fee explanation. "Cancel Anytime."
