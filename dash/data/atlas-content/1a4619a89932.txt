# 01 — First Reply: From Interest to Conversation

> **What this framework covers:** The moment a prospect replies to our first outreach email, through to one of three outcomes: a meeting booked, a qualification out, or a natural conversation conclusion.
>
> **Status:** Hypothesis v0.1 — built from principles, 0 conversations of evidence. Every section will be refined as data arrives.

---

## The Objective of the First Reply

**Don't sell. Don't pitch. Don't explain.** The first reply has one job: turn a reply into a conversation.

A prospect who replies to "A question about your quoting" has done something unusual — they've responded to a cold email that asked them to think. That's a signal. But it's not a buying signal. It's an attention signal. They're curious enough to engage. Now we have to earn the next exchange.

**The objective, stated plainly:**
> Get them to describe their current process in their own words, so they discover the gap themselves.

If they describe their process and realise it's inconsistent, person-dependent, or invisible — they've taken ownership of the problem. If we describe it for them ("you're losing track of quotes"), we're selling. The difference is everything.

**What the first reply is NOT trying to do:**
- Explain the product
- Share the Travis case study
- Ask for a meeting
- Ask for a demo
- Qualify them against our ideal customer profile (that comes after discovery)
- "Move them down the funnel"

**Success looks like:**
- They reply again — this time with more detail about their business
- They acknowledge a gap or inconsistency (even mildly: "yeah, it's not great")
- They ask a question about how it could work (not "what do you sell", but "how would you handle X")

**Failure looks like:**
- We jump to product and they go quiet
- We ask too many questions and they feel interrogated
- We respond with a wall of text and they mark it for later

## Common Response Types (and how to read them)

Every reply falls into one of these categories. How we respond depends on the category — not on a pre-written script.

### Type 1: The Engaged Reply
**What it looks like:**
> "Interesting question. We don't really have a system — it's mostly me remembering to follow up. What did you have in mind?"

**Signal:** High. They've acknowledged the gap and opened the door.

**Our move:** Don't walk through the door yet. Stay on their side of it. Ask ONE question about their current process. "Before I go into what we do — when you say it's mostly you remembering, what does that actually look like day to day?"

**Why:** If we jump to the product now, we answer a question they haven't fully formed. Let them sit with the gap a moment longer. The more they describe their current process, the more they'll want a better one.

### Type 2: The Process Reply
**What it looks like:**
> "We use a CRM for this. Sales team logs everything and it auto-sends reminders."

**Signal:** Neutral to warm. They've described their solution. This is either: (a) they genuinely have it covered, or (b) they have a system but don't fully trust it.

**Our move:** Genuine curiosity. "Nice — which CRM? And does it handle the full cycle from quote to follow-up, or is there still a bit of manual work involved?"

**Why:** If they have a solid system, they'll tell you and we'll qualify out gracefully. If there's a gap (the CRM does pipeline but not follow-up, or people forget to log), they'll reveal it. Either outcome is useful.

### Type 3: The Skeptical Reply
**What it looks like:**
> "Who is this? Are you selling something?"

**Signal:** Low trust. They replied — which is more than most — but they're on guard.

**Our move:** Direct, honest, zero pitch. "Fair question. I'm Atlas. I work with a small company called Not Forgotten — we help service businesses make sure no quote gets forgotten. I wasn't reaching out to pitch you, genuinely curious how you handle it. No expectations either way."

**Why:** Disarm with honesty. No clever reframing. No pivot. Just answer the question directly and leave the door open. If they don't reply, that's fine — they weren't ready. If they do, the trust barrier is lower.

### Type 4: The "Not Interested" Reply
**What it looks like:**
> "Thanks but we're good. We have this covered."

**Signal:** Neutral — polite decline.

**Our move:** Graceful exit. "Appreciate you letting me know. If anything changes or you ever want to compare notes on how other [industry] businesses handle this, I'm easy to find. All the best."

**Why:** Don't burn the bridge. Don't argue. Don't "overcome." A polite no today is a maybe in six months — but only if we leave a good impression now. Update the CRM, set a re-engagement reminder for 90 days.

### Type 5: The Bounce / Auto-Reply
**What it looks like:**
> "I'm out of the office until..." or a delivery failure notification.

**Signal:** Not a human reply. Process accordingly.

**Out of office:** Note in CRM. The follow-up sequence adjusts — don't burn Email 2 on an empty inbox. Wait for their return date + 2 days, then resume.

**Bounce:** Already handled. Mark Lost in CRM with bounce reason. Replace in campaign if experiment design requires it.

### Type 6: The Wrong Person Reply
**What it looks like:**
> "I'm not the right person for this — you should talk to [Name]."

**Signal:** Warm. They've given us a referral.

**Our move:** Thank them, ask for an intro if they offered, then reach out to the right person. "Thanks Sarah — really appreciate you pointing me in the right direction. I'll reach out to Mark directly. Would it be alright if I mention you suggested I get in touch?"

**Why:** Warm referral is gold. Don't waste it by sending a cold-style email to the new contact. This is now a warm introduction — different rules apply (see `knowledge/outreach-philosophy/07-when-to-break-the-rules.md`).

## Discovery Questions

Discovery is not an interrogation. It's a conversation where the prospect realises things about their own business. The best discovery question is the one they haven't been asked before.

### The Discovery Question Stack

Use these in order. Never ask more than one per message.

| Stage | Question | Purpose | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Process | "What does your current follow-up process look like day to day?" | They describe their reality | After they've engaged with the topic |
| 2. Visibility | "How do you know which quotes are still alive at any given moment?" | Surfaces the visibility gap — almost no one can answer this precisely | After they've described their process |
| 3. Consistency | "Does that process work the same way when you're busy versus when you're quiet?" | Reveals the person-dependency — busy periods are when follow-up breaks | After visibility, or if they claim to have a system |
| 4. Volume | "Roughly how many quotes go out each month?" | Quantifies the problem — small volume = small gap, large volume = large gap | Natural follow-up to consistency |
| 5. Cost | "Have you ever wondered about a quote that just went quiet — whether a follow-up might have changed things?" | Emotional connection to the loss | Late in discovery, after they've acknowledged gaps |

**Key rules:**
- One question per message. Always.
- Their answer determines the next question — don't pre-plan a sequence.
- If they answer with a question of their own ("How would you handle it?"), answer it — don't deflect with another question. That's not discovery, it's evasion.
- If they describe a solid system, acknowledge it genuinely. Don't try to find a gap that doesn't exist.

### What NOT to ask in discovery

| Don't ask | Why | Ask instead |
|---|---|---|
| "How much revenue are you losing?" | Assumptive. We don't know their numbers. | "Have you ever wondered about a quote that went quiet?" |
| "What's your budget?" | Too early. Feels transactional. | Not yet. Qualification covers this later. |
| "Who makes the decision?" | Feels like you're trying to go around them. | Not asked directly. Inferred from conversation. |
| "What's stopping you from fixing this?" | Assumes they want to fix it. They may not have named the problem yet. | Let them name it first. |

## Qualification

Qualification doesn't happen in the first reply. It happens across the first 2-3 exchanges. But the first reply sets it in motion.

### What We're Qualifying For

Not "can they pay?" — that's transaction-level qualification. We're qualifying for: **is this a business we can genuinely help, and are they someone we want to work with?**

| Dimension | Signal (positive) | Signal (negative) |
|---|---|---|
| **Quote volume** | 10+ quotes/month | <5 quotes/month — gap may be too small |
| **Process maturity** | Inconsistent, person-dependent, or absent | Solid CRM with automated follow-up — they don't need us |
| **Decision authority** | Replying person owns or manages the business | Assistant forwarding — may need different approach |
| **Problem awareness** | Acknowledges gap or inconsistency | Insists everything is fine (but replied anyway — there's something) |
| **Curiosity** | Asks questions about how it could work | Only answers questions, never asks |
| **Fit with our values** | Genuine, direct, no red flags | Aggressive, dismissive, or pushy |

### When to Qualify Out

Qualify out when:
- They genuinely have this covered (mature CRM, automated follow-up, consistent process)
- Quote volume is too low for the product to create meaningful value (<5/month)
- They're not the decision maker and can't/won't connect us
- Something feels off — trust your judgment, flag to James

**How to qualify out gracefully:**
> "Honestly, it sounds like you've got this pretty well sorted. Most businesses I talk to don't have anywhere near that level of process. If anything changes or you ever want to compare notes, I'm around."

Why: Leave the door open. A business that's well-organised today might not be tomorrow. And they might refer someone who isn't.

## Identifying Buying Intent

Buying intent is not a single signal. It's a pattern across multiple exchanges.

### Early Signals (first 1-2 exchanges)

| Signal | What it means | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| They reply more than once | The topic has their attention | Low — could just be politeness |
| They describe their process in detail | They're engaged with the question | Low-Medium |
| They ask "how would you handle this?" | They're connecting the gap to a solution | Medium |
| They volunteer pain ("it's a nightmare," "I've been meaning to fix this") | They've already named the problem to themselves | Medium |
| They respond quickly (<24 hours) | The topic is top of mind | Low-Medium |

### Stronger Signals (3-4 exchanges)

| Signal | What it means | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| They ask about pricing or how it works | They're evaluating | Medium-High |
| They describe a specific scenario ("what about when...") | They're imagining using it | High |
| They mention a timeframe ("we're looking at fixing this in the next month") | They have intent and urgency | High |
| They ask what the next step is | They want to move forward | High |

### False Positives

| Signal | Why it can mislead |
|---|---|
| Quick reply to first email | Could just be curiosity, not intent |
| "Tell me more" | May be polite brush-off if not followed by specific questions |
| "Interesting" with no follow-up | Acknowledgment, not intent |
| Asking about price early | Could be price-shopping, not evaluating |

**The rule:** Never act on a single signal. Look for patterns across exchanges. The strongest pattern: they describe their gap in detail + they ask a specific question about how it works + they reply quickly.

## Objection Handling

Objections in the first reply are rare (they just replied — they're curious, not resistant). But when they appear, handle them as discovery opportunities, not obstacles to overcome.

### Common Early Objections

| Objection | What it usually means | Our response |
|---|---|---|
| "We already have a CRM" | They think this is a CRM replacement | "Nice — which one? Most CRMs track pipeline but don't handle the follow-up conversation itself." |
| "We're too small for this" | They're underestimating their quote volume or the cost of inconsistency | "Fair. Roughly how many quotes go out each month? If it's only a handful, you're probably right. If it's more than you think, might be worth a look." |
| "I don't have time to look at this right now" | Genuine — they're busy. Or: they're not interested enough to prioritise. | "Totally understand. Want me to check back in a few weeks, or would you prefer I send you something short you can skim when you have a minute?" |
| "How much does it cost?" | Could be genuine evaluation or a polite way to end the conversation | "Happy to get to that. Before I do — can I ask one question to make sure this is even relevant to you?" |
| "We're happy with how we do things" | Defensive. They replied, so something made them curious. | "That's great — genuinely. I wasn't reaching out because I assumed you weren't. Just curious how you currently handle it." |

### The Objection Handling Principle

**Never overcome. Understand.**

"I'll think about it" isn't the real objection — it's a placeholder. "We have a CRM" might mean they love their CRM... or it might mean they're embarrassed they don't use it properly. "We're too small" might be true... or it might be modesty.

The job isn't to argue. It's to ask the question that reveals what's underneath.

## When Atlas Drafts vs When James Takes Over

This is the Tier 2 boundary applied to live conversation.

### Atlas Drafts (Tier 2 — draft and ask)

Atlas drafts ALL first replies. Every one. James reviews and approves before send.

Once a reply type has been handled successfully 3+ times with consistent quality, it can be nominated for Tier 1 promotion (auto-send). But we're at 0 — everything is draft-and-ask.

### James Takes Over

James takes over the conversation when:

| Trigger | Why |
|---|---|
| The prospect asks to speak with someone directly | They want the founder, not the system |
| The conversation reaches Rung 5 (call/demo) | James runs demos — Atlas schedules, doesn't run them |
| The prospect raises a complex or unusual scenario | Beyond Atlas's current judgment |
| The prospect expresses frustration, confusion, or anger | Requires human empathy and judgment |
| A pricing negotiation begins | James owns pricing decisions |
| The prospect is a warm referral from James's network | Different dynamic — James leads the relationship |
| Atlas is uncertain about the right move | If in doubt, escalate |

**The handoff protocol:**
1. Atlas identifies the trigger
2. Atlas drafts the transition message: "I'm going to loop in James, our founder — he's better placed to help with this. One moment."
3. Atlas presents the full conversation context to James: who, what's been said, what stage, what the trigger is, what Atlas would do if handling it
4. James takes over the conversation from there

### The Return Protocol

When James wants Atlas to resume:
1. James says "Atlas, take it from here" or equivalent
2. Atlas reads the new messages, continues the conversation from where James left off
3. Atlas maintains James's tone and positioning — don't revert to a different voice

## CRM Updates After Each Interaction

After every exchange, the CRM must reflect reality. This is non-negotiable.

### What to Update After a Reply Arrives

| Field | Update |
|---|---|
| `Pipeline stage` | Change from "Outreach Active" to "Engaged" |
| `Last contacted date` | Today's date |
| `Outreach notes` | Append: `[YYYY-MM-DD] Reply received — [category: Engaged/Process/Skeptical/Not Interested/Wrong Person]. [One-line summary of what they said.]` |

### What to Create

| Table | Record |
|---|---|
| `Messages` | New inbound Message: Direction=Inbound, Channel=Email, Body=their reply text, AI category=(categorise), Sent at=today |

### What to Update After Our Reply Goes Out

| Field | Update |
|---|---|
| `Last contacted date` | Today's date |
| `Outreach notes` | Append: `[YYYY-MM-DD] Reply sent — [one-line summary of our response]. Stage: [current stage].` |

### What to Create

| Table | Record |
|---|---|
| `Messages` | New outbound Message: Direction=Outbound, Channel=Email, Body=our reply text, Approval status=Approved, Sent at=today |

### When to Update Pipeline Stage

| Trigger | New Stage |
|---|---|
| First reply received | "Engaged" |
| Meeting booked | "Engaged" (add meeting details to notes) |
| Qualified out | "Campaign Complete" + reason in notes |
| Conversation naturally concludes (no reply in 14 days) | "Campaign Complete" + notes |
| Hard bounce or "never contact again" | "Lost" + reason |

### The Verification Check

After every CRM update, run a 30-second verification:
1. Does the prospect's `Pipeline stage` match the actual state of the conversation?
2. Does the `Last contacted date` match the date of the most recent Message?
3. Is there a Messages record for every exchange (both inbound and outbound)?

If any answer is no, fix it immediately. Don't let the CRM drift.

## The First Reply Decision Tree

```
PROSPECT REPLIES
    │
    ├─ Auto-reply / OOO → Note CRM, adjust timing, don't burn Email 2
    │
    ├─ Bounce → Mark Lost, log reason, replace if needed
    │
    ├─ Not interested → Graceful exit, CRM update, 90-day re-engagement
    │
    ├─ Wrong person → Thank, get intro, warm outreach to right person
    │
    ├─ Skeptical → Direct honest answer, zero pitch, leave door open
    │
    └─ Engaged / Process description →
            │
            └─ Categorise reply type
            └─ Draft response (Tier 2 — James approves)
            └─ One discovery question (not product, not pitch)
            └─ Update CRM: Stage → Engaged, create Messages record
            └─ Wait for next reply
                │
                ├─ They engage further → Continue discovery stack
                ├─ They ask about product → Answer, then return to discovery
                ├─ They go quiet → Day 5 follow-up (context-aware)
                └─ They qualify out → Graceful exit, CRM update
```
