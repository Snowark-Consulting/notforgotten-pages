## The Decision

All Not Forgotten Systems outreach and customer communication originates from atlas@notforgotten.com.au — not from james@notforgotten.com.au. Atlas has its own identity, signature, and presence. James remains the founder and face of the company, but the system sends the messages, not the person.

---

## The Problem

When a prospect receives an email, who are they building a relationship with — James the founder, or Not Forgotten the platform? If every email comes from James personally, the business is a consultancy pretending to be a SaaS. If the business is genuinely a platform, the platform should have its own voice.

The signature question surfaced this tension. The first draft used "James Hardstaff / Founder" with a mobile number. James corrected it to "Atlas / Growth Manager / W: notforgotten.com.au / Helping businesses turn more quotes into paid jobs." The correction wasn't cosmetic — it was a statement about what kind of business this is.

---

## Options Considered

| # | Option | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| A | james@ for everything | Personal, trust-building. Every email signed by the founder. Risk: doesn't scale, business depends on James's personal brand. |
| B | atlas@ for everything | System-first. Consistent voice. Scalable. Risk: less personal, lower trust initially, some prospects want human contact. |
| C | atlas@ for outreach, james@ for customer comms | Hybrid: new prospects get the system, existing customers get the founder. Risk: confusing identity, two voices to manage. |
| D | Rotate based on context | Complex, inconsistent. Risk: nobody knows who they're talking to. |

---

## Why This Path

Option B was chosen because Not Forgotten's core differentiator is the system, not the person. If James is sending every email, the business hasn't actually solved the problem it sells — that follow-up shouldn't depend on a single person being available.

The signature was the key moment. "Atlas / Growth Manager" tells prospects: there's a system behind this, not just a busy founder who'll forget to follow up. It's honest about what the product is. It sets expectations correctly.

James isn't disappearing. He'll still be on sales calls, handling escalations, making strategic decisions. But the day-to-day communications — the thing the product actually does — comes from Atlas. That's the product working as designed.

---

## Core Principles Applied

- **System over person** — The business shouldn't depend on any individual being available to function. This includes email. (Source: Philosophical Directive 8, 10)
- **We're asking businesses to trust us with their reputation** — An email from "Atlas / Growth Manager" must be as trustworthy as one from James himself. This raises the bar on quality. (Source: Philosophical Directive 9)
- **Atlas is a multiplier, not a replacement** — James is still the founder. Atlas handles the repeatable work so James can focus on what only he can do. (Source: Philosophical Directive 10)

---

## Risks Identified

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower open/reply rates from an unfamiliar sender name | Medium | Medium | Professional signature, consistent quality, build recognition over time. Track metrics and compare to industry benchmarks. |
| Prospects want to talk to the founder, not "the system" | Medium | Medium | James is available for calls and escalations. The email comes from Atlas, but the company is still human. |
| "Atlas" sounds too robotic/AI | Low | Low | Humanised tone. "Growth Manager" titles it as a role, not a bot. If it becomes a friction point, reconsider. |
| Signature too long or too revealing | Low | Low | Already iterated. Signature centres the brand URL and value proposition, not the individual. |

---

## Expected Outcome

Prospects build a relationship with Not Forgotten Systems, not with James personally. When James is unavailable, the system continues to communicate at the same standard. Open and reply rates are within acceptable range of personal-sender benchmarks. Zero prospects confused about who Atlas is after the first exchange.

---

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Open rates within 10% of industry benchmark for cold outreach from personal senders
- [ ] Reply rates within 10% of benchmark
- [ ] Zero prospects asking "who is Atlas?" in a way that suggests confusion rather than curiosity
- [ ] Signature used consistently across all outbound without modification
- [ ] James spends zero time sending routine follow-up emails personally

---

## Atlas's Challenge (Pre-Decision)

> The concern was raised during the signature iteration. James initially included his mobile number and used "James Hardstaff / Founder" — I pushed back because it contradicted the system-over-person principle and would create expectations that James is personally available to every prospect. James corrected the signature. The debate was brief but important: it established that even small details (a signature line) reflect strategic decisions.

**James's response:** Accepted. Removed personal mobile and name. Signature now reads: "Atlas / Growth Manager / W: notforgotten.com.au / Helping businesses turn more quotes into paid jobs."
