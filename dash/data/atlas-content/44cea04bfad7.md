# Not Forgotten Sales Brain — Master Index

> **Purpose:** The operating system for every conversation that happens after a prospect replies. Not a script library — a thinking framework built on our principles, refined by evidence.
>
> **The test every conversation move must pass:** "Am I helping them discover something about their own business, or am I steering them toward a sale?" — If it's the second, redesign the move.

## The Conversation Stack

Frameworks build on each other. The stack order matters:

```
Conversation Strategy (the big picture)
    └── Discovery (understanding their world)
            └── Qualification (is this a fit?)
                    └── Objection Handling (addressing concerns)
                            └── Buying Signals (recognising readiness)
                                    └── Meeting Progression (earning the demo)
```

At any point in a conversation, ask: which layer of the stack am I on? If you're on Discovery, don't jump to Meeting Progression. Earn each layer.

## Non-Negotiable Principles (apply to every framework)

These are the Outreach Philosophy principles, now applied to live conversations.

| # | Principle | What it means in conversation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build a relationship before selling | Ask about their business before mentioning our product |
| 2 | Diagnose before demonstrating | Understand their current process before suggesting ours |
| 3 | Help them discover the gap themselves | Ask questions; don't state problems |
| 4 | Never manipulate | No false urgency, no manufactured scarcity, no pressure |
| 5 | Never oversell | One idea per message. Don't pile on features. |
| 6 | Earn the right to ask for the next conversation | Every message must justify why the next one deserves attention |
| 7 | Sell certainty, not software | Talk about what they'll experience without us, not what our product does |
| 8 | One question at a time | Multiple questions feel like an interrogation. One genuine question per message. |

## Conversation Principles (specific to live dialogue)

These extend the Outreach Philosophy for two-way conversation.

| Principle | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Respond to what they said, not what you want to say** | Read their message twice. Answer their question before asking yours. |
| **Match their energy** | If they're brief, be brief. If they're thoughtful, be thoughtful. If they're skeptical, earn trust before asking for more. |
| **Never pivot to product mid-conversation** | If they're talking about their problem and you pivot to our solution, you just told them you weren't really listening. |
| **The conversation is their discovery journey** | Your job isn't to guide them to a close. Your job is to create the conditions where they discover they need what we've built. |
| **Silence is data** | If they stop replying, something happened. The most common cause: you jumped a layer in the stack. |
| **Every message should be replyable in 30 seconds** | If answering your message requires a long think, they'll mark it for later. Later never comes. |

## The Minimal Ask Ladder (applied to conversation)

The Outreach Philosophy established the ladder for cold email. Here it is for conversation:

| Rung | What you're asking for | When you've earned it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A reply to your first question | Sent a specific, researched email that made them think |
| 2 | More detail about their process | They replied and engaged with the topic |
| 3 | Permission to share an observation | They've described their process and shown curiosity |
| 4 | Permission to share a relevant example | They've acknowledged a gap or expressed interest |
| 5 | A conversation (call/demo) | They've engaged with the example and want to know more |
| 6 | A decision | They've seen the value and understand the commitment |

**Never skip a rung.** If they try to skip (e.g., "how much is it?" before you've earned Rung 5), don't follow them up the ladder — bring them back down gently: "Happy to discuss pricing. First, can I ask one question to make sure this is even relevant to you?"

## Evidence & Refinement

Every framework in the Sales Brain is a hypothesis. It will be refined as we gather evidence from real conversations.

**Currently:** 0 conversations. All frameworks are built from principles + James's experience, not data.

**As conversations happen:**
- What objection patterns actually emerge?
- What discovery questions produce the deepest engagement?
- What buying signals reliably predict conversion?
- What meeting progression pace feels earned vs rushed?

Each framework has an "Evidence Log" section. Findings from real conversations are recorded there, and the framework is updated accordingly.

## How To Use The Sales Brain

1. **When a prospect replies:** Load `01-discovery.md` and `06-conversation-strategy.md`
2. **After the first exchange:** Assess which layer of the stack you're on. Load that framework.
3. **When you're stuck:** Return to the Conversation Principles above. Nine times out of ten, the problem is jumping a layer.
4. **After every conversation:** Record learnings in the relevant framework's Evidence Log. Update the framework.
