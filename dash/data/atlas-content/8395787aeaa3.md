## 1. Click Click Media

### Final Email

**Subject:** A question about your quoting

Hi Click Click Media,

Your work covers SEO, PPC, and web design — different services, different scopes, different quoting processes.

Once a proposal leaves your hands, how do you track whether it got a reply?

I'm asking because I've noticed most agencies rely on whoever sent it remembering to follow up. That's a system that tends to break when things get busy.

Atlas

### Industry Context
Digital marketing agency, 10-30 people. Full-service: SEO, PPC, paid search, web design. Generalist positioning — the kind of agency where one person might be handling strategy, quoting, and delivery simultaneously. High likelihood that follow-up is person-dependent rather than process-dependent.

### Why This Opening
The observation about multiple service lines is true but not over-researched. The point isn't to impress them with what we know — it's to surface a genuine operational question: with different scopes going out across different services, how do you keep track?

### Why This Question
"Once a proposal leaves your hands, how do you track whether it got a reply?" — this is a question most agency owners have never been asked. It shifts attention from the quality of the proposal (something they're proud of) to what happens after (something they probably haven't systematised).

### What We Deliberately Left Out
- Travis case study (not relevant enough for agencies, save for follow-up)
- Product mention (principle #1: no pitch in first email)
- Their specific client industries or recent work (would read as trying too hard)

### Success Definition
Any reply. A "we have a CRM" or "we just follow up manually" or "interesting question" — all wins. The goal is to start a conversation, not to sell.

## 2. Online Marketing Gurus (OMG)

### Final Email

**Subject:** A question about your quoting

Hi Online Marketing Gurus,

You're working with over a thousand Australian businesses across SEO, PPC, social, and web development. That's a serious volume of proposals going out each month.

When a prospect goes quiet after receiving one, is there a standard follow-up process — or does it vary depending on which team sent it?

Curious how you handle this at your scale.

Atlas

Enterprise-scale digital marketing agency, 200+ team, 1,000+ clients (70-80% SMB). Full-service with recent acquisition of Rysen (UX/UI). Australia's "most awarded" positioning. At this scale, proposal volume is a genuine operational challenge — different teams, different services, different proposal formats.

### Why This Opening
The scale observation is specific to OMG without being sycophantic. "That's a serious volume of proposals" acknowledges their reality without praising them. The question about team-level variation is a genuine operational curiosity that someone at their scale would recognise instantly.

### Why This Question
"Is there a standard follow-up process — or does it vary depending on which team sent it?" — at 200+ people, this is not a trivial question. Different teams have different rhythms. The follow-up quality of SEO proposals vs web dev proposals vs social campaigns could be wildly inconsistent. This question names a problem they almost certainly have but may not have measured.

### What We Deliberately Left Out
- Their awards (50+ — would read as flattery)
- The Rysen acquisition (too inside-baseball, could seem stalker-ish)
- Travis case study (painting case study doesn't connect to agency world)
- Any mention of their SMB focus (70-80% stat feels like we read their About page too carefully)

### Success Definition
Any reply. At their scale, even a one-line acknowledgment is significant. If the reply names a specific pain point, that's the ideal outcome — it tells us exactly where to go in email 2.

## 3. Next&Co

### Final Email

**Subject:** A question about your quoting

Hi Next&Co,

Media buying, CRO, attribution — these take real work to scope and quote.

When a proposal goes out and the prospect doesn't reply, how do you handle the follow-up?

I'm asking because in service businesses like yours, my experience is that follow-up usually depends on whoever sent it remembering. Curious if that's the case at Next&Co.

Atlas

### Industry Context
Melbourne-based performance marketing agency, since 2010. "The Continuous Improvement Agency" — positioning built around accountability and measurable results. Services: digital strategy, media buying, organic search, CRO, CRM/loyalty, UX/CX design. 10-30 people. Work with Drive, Bitdefender, Bevilles, Doctors on Demand. Analytical, attribution-focused culture.

### Why This Opening
Listing three specific services (media buying, CRO, attribution) demonstrates we looked at them without over-selling the research. The observation that these "take real work to scope and quote" is a statement of respect for their process — it acknowledges the thinking time involved, which matters to an agency that brands on accountability.

### Why This Question
For an agency that positions on "continuous improvement" and "accountability," the question of quote follow-up process goes to their identity. If they have a strong process, they'll tell us proudly. If they don't, the question creates productive discomfort — a gap between their stated values and their actual operations.

### What We Deliberately Left Out
- Their specific clients (Drive, Bitdefender, etc.) — name-dropping reads as research flexing
- "The Continuous Improvement Agency" tagline (too inside-baseball)
- "Measurably Better™" trademark reference (would seem like we're obsessively researching them)
- Any comparison to other agencies (never position prospects against peers)

### Success Definition
A reply that engages with the follow-up question. If they say "we have a process" — that's an opening to ask what it is. If they say "we don't really have one" — that's an even better opening.

## Experimental Notes

### WebAlive Replacement (2026-06-29)

**Decision:** WebAlive (`info@webalive.com.au`) removed from calibration campaign and replaced with Next&Co (`enquiries@nextandco.com.au`).

**Rationale:** Pre-send verification identified potential ownership, infrastructure, or contact overlap between WebAlive and Online Marketing Gurus (Supergravity Group). Including both risked experimental contamination in three ways:
1. **Infrastructure overlap** — shared email systems, domains, or backend would produce correlated delivery/receipt patterns
2. **Decision-maker overlap** — if they share ownership, a reply from one could be the same person replying from the other
3. **Data contamination** — non-independent data points invalidate reply-rate comparisons across verticals

**Selection criteria for replacement:**
- Must have no detectable ownership, infrastructure, or contact overlap with OMG
- Must preserve the three-agency diversity within the agency vertical
- Must have Qualification score 9 (same quality tier as WebAlive)
- Must have a working, loadable website for genuine research

**Next&Co selected because:**
- Independently verified: Melbourne-based, no connection to OMG/Supergravity detected
- Distinct agency profile: performance/attribution specialist vs OMG's volume/awards-driven approach
- Different geography: Melbourne vs OMG Sydney / CCM national
- Clean website, genuine About page, real case studies
- Preserves the experiment's variable isolation: three agencies, three profiles, no correlated data

**Lesson for future campaigns:** Before finalising any multi-prospect campaign in the same vertical, run an ownership/infrastructure overlap check. Two prospects under the same parent company or shared backend produce correlated results — not independent data points.

### Pre-Flight Integrity Check (New SOP — 2026-06-29)

Before any outreach campaign is sent, Atlas will perform a standard integrity check:

1. **Airtable verification** — every prospect exists, Pipeline stage is correct, all required fields populated
2. **CRM readiness** — Messages table accessible, `crm-update-send.py` tested and operational
3. **Duplicate check** — no two prospects share a domain, redirect, parent company, or owner
4. **Ownership overlap check** — cross-reference business registrations, parent companies, shared infrastructure
5. **Experiment design validity** — variable isolation confirmed, no confounding factors introduced since design
6. **Supporting systems** — Gmail API authenticated, Airtable API responding, scripts operational
