# Decision: QPaint Daily Operations Model — Review → Decide (never Review → Send)

**Slug:** `2026-08-20-qpaint-daily-ops-model`
**Date:** 2026-08-20
**Status:** Made
**Review Date:** 2026-11-20

---

## The Decision

QPaint book control is accepted as **PASS** (167 records, 167 accounted for, every open quote intentionally managed). Going forward, QPaint returns to normal daily operations governed by one rule: **a review date means REVIEW → DECIDE, never REVIEW → SEND.**

Each customer is assessed individually at their review date using the full Customer Context Gate and current evidence. The correct next action is selected from: EMAIL / SMS / PHONE / WAIT / MONITOR / HOLD / QPAINT INPUT / FOUNDER ACTION / CLOSE WON / CLOSE LOST. No batch sends, no automatic Email 3, no queue processing merely because a date became due.

---

## The Problem

The previous operating mode was cadence-driven: a review date triggered a send by default. This produced repetitive touches that did not respect the customer's actual commercial journey, and a book-control review surfaced 22 stale records, 6 historical quotes in a fresh cadence, and two QPaint escalations framed as "awaiting" rather than "surfaced → monitor". The fix is not *less* follow-up; it is *better* follow-up.

---

## Options Considered

| # | Option | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| A | Cadence-driven send queue | Review date = send date. Batch processing. Repetitive, disrespects journey. Rejected. |
| B | Review → Decide (individual) | Review date = assess individually via CCG, then pick the correct action from the full menu. **Chosen.** |
| C | Two-touch stop rule | Stop after two unanswered emails. Rejected — persistence is part of the product; no artificial touch limit. |

---

## Why This Path

Option B preserves persistence while eliminating repetition. Some customers legitimately require a 3rd, 4th or 5th touch where commercial context supports it. The distinction is not "how many touches" but "does each touch have a reason". Persistence is still part of the product; pestering is not. This aligns with "Sell certainty, not follow-up" and the Customer Context Gate.

---

## Core Principles Applied

- Evidence before opinion (Six Principles #2) — each touch decided on the customer's actual journey, not the calendar
- Trust before growth (Six Principles #1) — a natural, well-timed touch builds trust; a repetitive one erodes it
- Human accountability (Six Principles #6) — James owns the follow-up decisions; Atlas advises and drafts
- Persistence ≠ repetition (founder directive)
- SURFACE ≠ MANAGE (role boundary, prior decision `2026-08-20-qpaint-role-boundary`)

---

## Before Recommending Another Touch — Six Questions

1. What is actually happening in this customer's journey?
2. What are we trying to achieve with this touch?
3. Does this channel still make sense?
4. Are we adding anything useful, or simply repeating ourselves?
5. Has enough time passed, given this type of customer and quote?
6. Would this communication feel natural if a good salesperson sent it?

---

## Daily Commercial Review Scope

Do **not** dump the whole book. Surface only:

- Customers requiring a decision today
- New replies
- New quotes/intakes requiring judgment
- Overdue customer commitments
- Genuine exceptions/anomalies
- Customers where another touch may now add commercial value

Each item reported with: evidence, commercial state, objective, recommended next action, and why that action makes sense now.

---

## Risks Identified

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Over-correction into under-following | Med | High | Persistence remains in scope; the six questions gate, they do not suppress |
| Review date treated as a suggestion and skipped | Med | Med | Book control baseline; every review date produces a deliberate DECIDE (even if the decision is WAIT/MONITOR) |
| Silent cohort still batch-processed by habit | Med | Med | No batch sends; each review is individual |

---

## Expected Outcome

Fewer, better touches. Customers who need persistence get it; customers whose journey is complete are left alone. QPaint sees higher-quality intelligence, not higher-volume contact. No repetitive "just checking in" emails.

---

## Success Criteria

- [ ] No email sent purely because "Next Touchpoint Date = today" without a Customer Context Gate decision
- [ ] Every additional touch has a stated commercial reason (what it achieves, why now)
- [ ] Daily review surfaces only decision-needed items, not the full book
- [ ] QPaint input raised only where genuine customer evidence requires their involvement

## Learnings

Cadence should create a *review*, not a *send*. The product promise — "no opportunity forgotten" — is delivered by reliable, well-judged follow-up, not by maximum-touch output. Persistence is a tool; repetition is a failure of judgment. The system's job is to know the difference.
