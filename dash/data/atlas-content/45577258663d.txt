# Decision: Calibration Outreach Strategy as Living System

## The Decision

The calibration outreach strategy is adopted as our baseline. Three meta-principles govern its operation: (1) the strategy is a **living system**, not a fixed playbook — when data contradicts assumptions, we challenge our own thinking and adapt; (2) **ending a campaign does not end a relationship** — 4 emails may be the right place to stop a sequence, but genuine reconnection opportunities months later should be recognised and acted on; (3) **context-awareness overrides timetables** — if something changes with a business, their industry, or their circumstances, we adapt the strategy rather than blindly following a schedule.

---

## The Problem

The calibration outreach strategy needed formal adoption as our operating baseline. Without explicit principles governing adaptability, re-engagement, and context-awareness, the system risked mechanically following sequences without intelligence — exactly the kind of impersonal automation that contradicts our values. The core tension: efficiency says "follow the sequence," but trust says "respond to the situation."

---

## Options Considered

| # | Option | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| A | Fixed playbook | 4 emails, hard stop, prospect marked as closed. Efficient but treats every prospect identically. |
| B | Living system (chosen) | 4-email campaign as baseline. Data drives adaptation. Campaign end ≠ relationship end. Context-aware overrides. |

---

## Why This Path

This path aligns with every founding principle. System-over-person: the knowledge compounds with every interaction. Earn the right: reconnection requires genuine relevance, not persistence. Principles before speed: intelligence over efficiency. The trade-off is operational complexity — a living system requires more judgment than a fixed playbook. That's a feature, not a bug. It's why Atlas exists.

---

## Core Principles Applied

- **System over person** — Every reply, non-reply, and interaction is training data that makes the system better
- **Earn the right** — Reconnection is earned by genuine relevance to the prospect's situation, not by persistence
- **Customer trust > speed** — Context-awareness builds trust; blind automation destroys it
- **Certainty, not features** — The goal is not to send emails. It's to build trust, start conversations, and continuously improve from evidence

---

## Risks Identified

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Living system becomes "no system" — adaptability becomes an excuse for inconsistency | Med | High | Documented baseline strategy; all deviations must be justified and recorded |
| Re-engagement without genuine reason feels like spam | Low | High | "Genuine reason" requirement is non-negotiable; re-engagement triggers must be defined and approved |
| Context-awareness requires monitoring we don't yet have | High | Med | Start manual (research before each touch); build monitoring incrementally as patterns emerge |
| Campaign-vs-relationship distinction is lost in Airtable — prospects marked "lost" prematurely | Med | Med | Need a "Campaign Complete" status distinct from "Closed/Lost" (see open question below) |

---

## Expected Outcome

- Reply rates tracked per campaign and per industry, feeding back into strategy
- At least one assumption challenged and revised based on data within 90 days
- Re-engagement framework designed before first calibration campaign completes
- No prospect is permanently "closed" — only "campaign complete, awaiting genuine reconnection trigger"

---

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Reply rate tracked and reported per campaign (within 30 days of first send)
- [ ] Pattern analysis per industry initiated (within 60 days)
- [ ] At least one data-driven change to strategy proposed and discussed (within 90 days)
- [ ] Re-engagement trigger framework documented (before first campaign completes)
- [ ] No prospect mistakenly treated as "closed forever" due to campaign end
