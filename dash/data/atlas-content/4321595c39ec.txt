# Website Strategy Review — Not Forgotten Systems

**Date:** 5 August 2026
**Author:** Atlas — Strategic Review
**Status:** ⏳ Pending James Approval
**Scope:** Complete homepage audit + recommendations
**Live URL:** https://notforgotten.com.au

> This is a brutal critique. Nothing is protected. Every section is reviewed against what Not Forgotten has actually become — not what it was positioned as when the site launched.

---

## Executive Summary

**The website positions Not Forgotten as a quote follow-up tool. The business has become an AI-powered Customer Communication Operating System.**

The gap between the website and the product is now significant enough that it undersells the business to every visitor. The messaging is feature-led ("automated follow-up") when it should be outcome-led ("money you're leaving on the table, recovered"). The website would be competitive in April 2026. In August 2026, after today's evidence of a $129,750 recovery from a 365-day dormant quote, it's behind.

**The one-sentence verdict:** "Interesting AI tool for quote follow-up" — not "This fundamentally changes how companies manage customer communication."

---

## Section-by-Section Review

### 1. Page Title & Meta Description

**Current:**
> Title: "Not Forgotten Systems | Automated Quote Follow-Up for Your Business"
> Meta: "We make sure no quote is forgotten. Automated follow-up by email and SMS. $177K recovered for one business. $199/month. Start today."

**Critique:**
The title immediately positions NF as a "quote follow-up" company. This was accurate in April. It is no longer accurate. Not Forgotten recovers revenue, generates business intelligence, manages customer relationships, and continuously learns from every interaction. Quote follow-up is the mechanism — not the value.

The meta description leads with "automated follow-up" (a feature) and buries the outcome ($177K recovered) in the middle. The $177K figure is also outdated — we now have $129,750 from a single QPaint deal in one day, plus active opportunities exceeding $330K.

**Recommendation:**
> Title: "Not Forgotten Systems | Revenue Recovery & Customer Communication"
> Meta: "We recover revenue from quotes that went cold. $129,750 recovered from a single dormant quote. Real customer care, real business intelligence, real results. $199/month."

---

### 2. Hero Section — H1 & Subhead

**Current:**
> H1: "Never lose a job because a quote went cold."
> Subhead: "CC us on your quotes. We follow up by email and SMS until your customer makes a decision. You focus on the work. The follow-up takes care of itself."

**Critique:**
The H1 is good — it speaks to pain. But "never lose a job because a quote went cold" is still a feature promise (we'll follow up). The subhead is entirely mechanistic: "CC us on your quotes" describes *how it works*, not *what changes for the business owner.*

This is the most valuable real estate on the entire site. It currently describes a process. It should describe a transformation.

**Recommendation:**
> H1: "Your quotes are worth more than you think."
> Subhead: "We recovered $129,750 from a quote that sat dormant for a year. Not by chasing harder. By communicating better. Every quote you've sent is an opportunity still in play — we make sure none of them are forgotten."

The H1 reframes from "we prevent loss" to "there's money you don't know about." The subhead leads with evidence — a specific number, a specific timeframe — then explains the mechanism. This is the pattern Arno Hsu proved today.

---

### 3. Social Proof Bar (Below Hero)

**Current:**
> "$177K+ Recovered by One Business" / "$59K Single Client in 3 Weeks" / "Jobs Recovered Jan-Apr 2026"

**Critique:**
The $177K figure is the Travis Krieg case study — strong, but old. The $59K figure is undated and unattributed. "Jobs Recovered Jan-Apr 2026" is vague.

We now have fresher, more specific evidence: Arno Hsu — $129,750, 365-day dormant quote, won same day as first contact. That's a more compelling stat than $177K over four months because it's specific, recent, and every business owner understands the pain of a year-old quote.

**Recommendation:**
Replace with three rotating or static stats:
> "$129,750 — Single dormant quote, recovered in hours" / "$177,555 — Painting business, 4 months" / "4 active deals — $330K+ in play"

Fresh, specific, varied.

---

### 4. ROI Calculator

**Current:**
> "See What You're Leaving On The Table" — slider-based calculator showing estimated recovery.

**Critique:**
The concept is strong — an interactive tool that shows value before asking for commitment. But the copy wrapping it is weak. "See What You're Leaving On The Table" is a good line. The calculator output ($8,119/mo, 50x ROI) is compelling.

The problem: it's positioned as a calculator, not as evidence. The Travis Krieg reference below it ("Travis Krieg Painting recovered $177,555") reads as an afterthought. The calculator estimates what *could* happen. The Travis figure proves what *did* happen. The hierarchy is inverted.

**Recommendation:**
Keep the calculator. Add a line directly above the output:
> "This isn't theoretical. Travis Krieg Painting recovered $177,555. QPaint recovered $129,750 from a single email. Your numbers will be different — but the pattern holds."

---

### 5. How It Works

**Current:**
> "Never Wonder What Happened To A Quote Again"
> Three steps: Send Your Quote → Automated Follow-Up → Customer Responds & You Get Notified → Monthly Insights

**Critique:**
This section describes a workflow. The problem is that it describes *our* workflow — CC us, we follow up, you get notified — not the customer's transformation. "Send Your Quote" is an instruction. "Automated Follow-Up Begins" is a feature. "Customer Responds & You Get Notified" is obvious.

The fourth step — "Monthly Insights Show You The Patterns" — is buried as an afterthought. This is actually one of NF's most differentiated capabilities. The business intelligence angle (what we learned today about customer objections, reply patterns, dormant quote conversion) is completely absent from the How It Works section.

**Recommendation:**
Reframe as outcomes, not steps:

> **1. Your quotes stop going cold.** Every quote enters a recovery sequence. Professional, human follow-up — not automated spam.
>
> **2. You learn what your customers are actually thinking.** Why did they say no? What made them say yes? Every reply becomes intelligence.
>
> **3. Revenue you didn't know was there comes back.** Dormant quotes. Forgotten follow-ups. Customers who needed one more touch. We find them.
>
> **4. You get a monthly report that reads like a business consultant wrote it.** Patterns. Trends. What to stop doing. What to do more of.

This is the same information, reframed from "here's how our tool works" to "here's what changes for your business."

---

### 6. Monthly Insights Report

**Current:**
> "You also get a monthly insights report" — sample report mockup showing quote outcomes, trends, recommendations.

**Critique:**
This is one of the strongest sections on the page and it's positioned as an add-on: "You also get." The mockup is excellent — it shows real data, real recommendations, real value. But the section label "Included With Your Plan" makes it feel like a bonus feature, not a core value proposition.

The business intelligence capability is what separates NF from every "automated follow-up" tool in the market. Most tools send emails. NF learns from the replies. That's the difference. The website doesn't communicate this.

**Recommendation:**
Move this section higher. Position it as a primary value proposition, not an add-on:
> H2: "Every customer conversation teaches you something. We capture it."
> "Most follow-up tools send emails and hope. We analyse every reply — why customers say yes, why they say no, what patterns repeat. Then we tell you. Every month. In plain English. No dashboard required."

---

### 7. Free Quote Health Check

**Current:**
> "Not Ready To Commit? Get a free Quote Health Check" — mailto link to quotes@notforgotten.com.au

**Critique:**
James has explicitly asked for this to be removed. Beyond that: it's a low-friction CTA that converts visitors into leads, but it also positions NF as "free analysis" rather than "paid service that pays for itself." The mailto link is clunky — it dumps the visitor into their email client with no tracking, no follow-up, no automation.

The concept (low-friction entry point) is sound. The execution (mailto, free report, "not ready to commit") undersells the business.

**Recommendation (James-directed):**
Remove the Health Check section entirely. Replace with a stronger primary CTA. See CTA section below.

### 8. Testimonials / Social Proof

**Current:**
> Travis Krieg testimonial + Founder origin story + "$177,555 recovered" stat card + case study link

**Critique:**
This section is solid but thin. One testimonial. One case study link. The founder story is good — "After 12 years in sales..." — but it reads as a personal narrative rather than proof that the system works.

We now have evidence we didn't have when this was written: 7 customer replies in one day. 6 BK principles created from real interactions. A $129,750 recovery from a single email. The section should reflect this.

**Recommendation:**
Add a second testimonial slot (reserved for QPaint/Tas when approved). Add a stat row:
> "7 customer replies in one day. 1 job won. 8 relationships preserved. 6 new principles learned. This is what happens when follow-up becomes a system."

### 9. Pricing

**Current:**
> "$199/month. $99 One-Time Setup Fee." — single plan, feature list, setup explanation.

**Critique:**
The pricing is clean and simple — no tiers, no upsells. That's a strength. But the positioning around it is wrong. "No Tiers. No Upsells. One Plan." is defensive — it reads as "we're not like those other SaaS companies." The feature list ("Automated quote follow-up, Email follow-up sequences, Customer response tracking, Monthly business insights report") describes capabilities, not outcomes.

Nowhere does it say: "This plan has already recovered $129,750 for one client. It pays for itself 650 times over."

**Recommendation:**
Keep the price. Keep the simplicity. Change the framing:
> "One plan. $199/month. $99 setup. No contracts. No surprises."
> Below the price: "QPaint recovered $129,750 in their first week. That's 650x the monthly cost. Your results will vary — but the economics are clear."

---

### 10. Industries

**Current:**
> "Built For Businesses That Quote" — Trades & Contractors, Home Services, Professional Services, Growing Businesses.

**Critique:**
This section is placeholder-quality. Four category cards with no differentiation, no industry-specific proof, no tailored messaging. Every industry gets the same generic treatment. The section communicates "we work with anyone who sends quotes" — which is true but not compelling.

**Recommendation:**
Replace with evidence-backed industry positioning. For now, lead with the strongest vertical (painting, given QPaint evidence):
> "We've recovered $307,305 for painting businesses alone. Your industry has its own patterns — we learn them."
> Then: "Trades & Contractors · Home Services · Professional Services" — each with a one-line outcome, not a feature.

---

### 11. About / Founder Story

**Current:**
> "Built By Someone Who Understands Sales" — James's 12-year background, manual recovery of $150K, built NF to solve the problem at scale.

**Critique:**
This section is good — authentic, personal, credible. But it reads as "here's why I started this company" rather than "here's why you should trust this company." The story is about James's journey. The visitor wants to know: "Will this work for my business?"

**Recommendation:**
Keep the founder story. Add one line that bridges James's experience to the visitor's situation:
> "I've sat where you're sitting — wondering which quotes are still alive, which ones died quietly, and how much money you've left on the table. I built Not Forgotten because I got tired of guessing."

### 12. CTAs — The Primary Call-to-Action Problem

**Current:**
- Primary: "Start Quote Follow-up" (opens booking modal)
- Secondary: "Get a Free Quote Health Check" (scrolls to health check section)
- Both repeated 4+ times throughout the page

**Critique:**
"Start Quote Follow-up" is a feature CTA. It asks the visitor to *start using the tool*. The Health Check CTA is being removed per James's direction.

Neither CTA communicates what the visitor actually wants: more revenue, less stress, better visibility.

**Recommendation:**
Replace both with a single primary CTA:
> **"See What Your Quotes Are Worth"**

This reframes from "start using our tool" to "discover value you didn't know existed." It's outcome-led, curiosity-driven, and doesn't commit the visitor to anything.

Variations for different sections:
- Hero: "See What Your Quotes Are Worth"
- Pricing: "Start Recovering Revenue"
- Bottom: "Book a Demo — 15 Minutes"

All lead to the same booking modal.

---

### 13. Navigation

**Current:**
> How It Works | Results | Pricing | Industries | "Start Follow-up"

**Critique:**
"Start Follow-up" as a nav CTA is a feature command. The nav labels describe the page structure, not the visitor's journey. "How It Works" is weak — every SaaS site has a "How It Works" link.

**Recommendation:**
> How It Works → **"What You Get"**
> Results → **"Proof"**
> "Start Follow-up" → **"See Your Quotes"**

Shorter. More curious. Less SaaS-generic.

---

### 14. Trust Signals

**Current:**
- Travis Krieg testimonial (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)
- "$177,555 recovered" stat
- Founder story
- Privacy policy link in footer

**Critique:**
The trust signals are thin. There's no security reassurance. No data privacy commitment on-page. No "how we handle your customer data." The testimonial is from one person. The founder story is personal but not institutional.

For a product that handles customer communications — effectively reading and responding to a business's customer emails — the trust bar is high. The website doesn't meet it.

**Recommendation:**
Add a trust section before the footer:
> **"Your customer relationships are safe with us."**
> - We never contact your customers without your approval
> - Every message is sent on your behalf, from your identity
> - Your quote data stays confidential — we don't share, sell, or mine it
> - Australian-owned. Australian-hosted. Australian privacy laws.

This addresses the unspoken fear every business owner has: "What if this AI says something wrong to my customer?"

---

### 15. Mobile Experience

**Critique:**
The site is responsive (single-column layout, hamburger nav). But the ROI calculator is likely cumbersome on mobile — sliders are inherently desktop-first. The monthly insights mockup is an image that may not scale well. The Health Check mailto link opens a desktop email client which is poor UX on mobile.

**Recommendation:**
Test on mobile. Replace sliders with input fields + tap targets. Replace mailto with a form. Ensure the insights mockup is legible at 375px width.

### 16. What's Missing Entirely

The following capabilities exist in the product but are absent from the website:

| Capability | Why It Matters | Where It Should Go |
|---|---|---|
| **Business Intelligence** — NF learns from every reply, identifies patterns, creates principles | Differentiates from every "automated follow-up" tool | Hero section or dedicated section before How It Works |
| **Customer Care** — white-label, human-sounding, acknowledgment protocol | This is the Arno Hsu story. Customers say "thank you." | How It Works or Testimonials |
| **Revenue Recovery Timeline** — specific, dated evidence of money recovered | Proof that beats any calculator | Results / Proof section |
| **Evidence Dossiers** — living documents that track every outcome | Institutional-grade transparency. No other tool does this. | Results or About section |
| **Multi-Client Architecture** — designed for 100 clients, not just one | Enterprise readiness signal | About or Trust section |
| **Production Standards** — email verification, reconciliation, incident response | Operational maturity. We don't just "send emails." | Trust section |
