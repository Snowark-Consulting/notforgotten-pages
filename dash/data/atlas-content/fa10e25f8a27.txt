## Email 1 — The Pattern

**Subject:** Something I've noticed

Hi {{First Name}},

Over the last few months I've spoken with a lot of painting business owners.

Different businesses. Different sizes. Different parts of Australia.

But one thing keeps coming up.

Everyone knows they should follow up their quotes. Almost nobody does it consistently.

Not because they don't care. Because they're busy.

They're quoting the next job. Managing staff. Ordering materials. Trying to get home before dark.

Follow-up ends up happening when someone remembers.

Sometimes they do. Sometimes they don't.

I've experienced exactly the same thing myself, and after talking with so many tradies, I've realised it isn't an individual problem. It's just what happens when you're running a busy business.

I'm curious...

Do you know what happens to the quotes you don't hear back about?

## Email 2 — The Conversation

**Subject:** I didn't expect this

Hi {{First Name}},

One conversation from last week has been stuck in my head.

A painter told me he thought he had a lead problem.

After we talked for a while, it turned out he didn't. He had a follow-up problem.

He was already quoting enough work. He just wasn't consistently hearing back from the people he'd already spent time quoting.

That made me wonder...

How many businesses think they need more leads...

...when what they really need is to convert more of the opportunities they've already earned?

Have you ever looked at it that way?

## Email 3 — The Evidence

**Subject:** One conversation I keep coming back to

Hi {{First Name}},

There's one conversation I keep coming back to.

Travis runs a painting business in Adelaide.

He wasn't looking for more leads. He wasn't trying to grow faster.

He simply wanted to stop wondering what happened to the quotes he'd already sent.

Over four months...

14 jobs came back. $180,115 in recovered work.

That wasn't the interesting part.

The interesting part was discovering those customers hadn't disappeared. Most of them had simply never been followed up.

That changed the way I think about quoting businesses.

Has anything similar ever happened in your business?

## Email 4 — The Reflection

**Subject:** One final question

Hi {{First Name}},

I've enjoyed sharing a few observations with you over the past couple of weeks.

Whether Not Forgotten is ever the right fit isn't actually the question I've been thinking about. The question I've been thinking about is this.

If nothing changes over the next 12 months...

Will you be happy with what happens to the quotes that never reply?

Not whether your business survives. I'm sure it will. Your work speaks for itself.

But will you ever wonder how many opportunities quietly disappeared simply because life got busy?

Maybe you've already solved that problem. If you have, that's fantastic.

But if not...

I hope the next time you send a quote, you ask yourself one simple question.

"What happens if I never hear back?"

That question changed the way I think about running a business.

If you'd ever like to have a conversation about it, I'd genuinely enjoy the chat.

## Experiment Philosophy

Improvements come from controlled experiments, not rewriting:

1. **Observation** — What happened?
2. **Evidence** — What data supports it?
3. **Hypothesis** — Why do we think it happened?
4. **Experiment** — What single change should we test?
5. **Success metric** — How will we know if it worked?

One experiment at a time. Measure. Learn. Repeat.
