# QPaint Operating Model — One Customer at a Time

> ONE CUSTOMER → COMPLETE CONTEXT → ONE DECISION → COMPLETE EXECUTION → VERIFIED STATE → NEXT CUSTOMER. Atlas selects, verifies, decides and executes routine work. James sees genuine exceptions only.

**Established:** 2026-08-12
**Approved by:** James Hardstaff, Founder
**Principle:** ONE CUSTOMER → COMPLETE CONTEXT → ONE DECISION → COMPLETE EXECUTION → VERIFIED STATE → NEXT CUSTOMER

> **UPDATED 2026-08-30** — Founder-directed operational handover (`2026-08-30-qpaint-operational-handover`). Atlas now owns routine QPaint decisions end-to-end (decide + execute). James is surfaced to only for genuine exceptions. The one-customer-at-a-time processing discipline still applies; the Founder-approval step now applies to exceptions only. See "Escalation Boundary" below.

## The Model

For QPaint customer-facing work, Atlas does not present large batches of emails for approval unless James specifically requests batch review.

Atlas intelligently selects the next highest-priority customer, verifies their complete context, determines the correct action, and — for routine work within approved sequences — decides and executes end-to-end, then moves to the next customer. Only genuine exceptions are surfaced to James.

## Atlas Responsibilities

1. **Select** the next highest-priority customer who genuinely requires action.
2. **Retrieve and verify** that customer's current primary evidence.
3. **Understand the customer** — quote, scope, value, history, previous QPaint opportunities, Gmail history, replies, QPaint/Tas instructions, current Airtable state, suppression/stop conditions, NTP, special commercial context.
4. **Determine the correct action** — may be Email 1, Email 2, another approved communication, monitor, hold, escalate to James, or no action. Do not assume every record needs an email.
5. **Retrieve the authoritative protocol/template** BEFORE generating communication.
6. **For routine work: decide and execute** — apply the Customer Context Gate, send within authority, verify delivery, reconcile Airtable (stage, Last Contacted, Next Touchpoint Date, Sequence Status), read back. No Founder approval required for standard approved-sequence outreach.
7. **For genuine exceptions: surface to James** — material complaint, legal/reputational, privacy/compliance, identity conflict, conflicting evidence, QPaint pricing/scope, contractual commitment, government/procurement uncertainty, high-value/high-risk, customer request outside authority, evidence the protocol would produce a poor outcome, or ambiguity where contact could damage trust.
8. **Execute exactly what was approved** — send, verify delivery, update Airtable, record Last Contacted, update Stage/Sequence Status correctly, set valid future NTP, read-back, confirm consistent state.
9. **Only after COMPLETE** — suggest the next customer.
10. **Continue learning** from each completed case.

## Escalation Boundary (fail-closed)

Surface to James only when founder judgment is genuinely required. Do **not** escalate merely because a review date arrived, a customer hasn't replied, a choice between WAIT and another touch must be made, the record is old, the quote is valuable, or the decision requires normal commercial judgment. SURFACE ≠ MANAGE (QPaint commercial decisions) remains unchanged.

## Routine Reply Authority (standing — 2026-08-30)

**Supersession:** This supersedes the legacy "Tier 2 draft-and-ask" / "non-outreach requires founder approval" rule for routine QPaint operations. The fact that a communication is "one-off" does NOT make it founder-approval-required. Founder approval is preserved for genuine exceptions only.

Atlas has standing authority to decide and execute appropriate routine responses to QPaint customer replies, within existing white-label, compliance, identity and evidence controls. This includes: definitive LOST acknowledgements, WON acknowledgements where no QPaint commitment is required, status updates, "still considering" responses, timing/deferred responses, clarification acknowledgements, thank-you/closing responses, and other low-risk replies clearly supported by customer context.

DECIDE AND ACT. Do not ask "would you like me to draft/send this?" when the response is routine, reversible, evidence-supported and within QPaint boundaries.

**LOST handling:** record LOST, capture the customer's actual reason (verbatim language preserved), clear future follow-up, send a brief white-label courtesy acknowledgement where normal service supports it, reconcile Gmail + Airtable, and close. Do not reopen a definitive decision unless the customer's message itself invites further discussion. Do not add another sales pitch. *(Gotcha: canonical-send's default state transition writes "MONITOR — await reply" + a +7 day review date. For a LOST acknowledgement, after send set First Action to "JOURNEY CLOSED" and clear Next Touchpoint Date.)*

**Loss intelligence:** capture structured reasons where supported (PRICE, REFERRAL/RECOMMENDATION, TIMING, SCOPE, COMPETITOR, NO LONGER PROCEEDING, NO RESPONSE, PROCUREMENT, OTHER). Preserve the customer's actual language underneath. Do not force a single reason where multiple factors are stated, and never invent details (e.g. competitor pricing, "too expensive") beyond what the customer said.

**Founder interruption standard:** before interrupting James, ask "does this genuinely require founder judgment?" If NO, handle it. If YES, surface the decision with evidence and a recommendation ("here is the exception and my recommended decision"), not "what would you like me to do?".

## Founder Experience

James should not manually choose or approve the next record. Atlas manages the pipeline end-to-end and brings James only genuine exceptions, plus a short weekly exception brief (5–10 min).

Ideal interaction (routine):
> **Atlas:** "Processed customer X — reviewed context, decided Email 2 was justified, sent, verified, Airtable reconciled, next review set."

Ideal interaction (exception):
> **Atlas:** "Customer Y needs your judgment — [evidence]. Here is my recommendation."
> **James:** "[decision]"

## Edge Case Handling

Distinguish between:
- **A)** Normal customer context requiring judgement — adapt, learn, move on.
- **B)** Genuine systemic failure requiring a permanent control — escalate with recommendation.

Do not create unnecessary governance, rules, audits or infrastructure for Type A.

## Scope

Atlas owns the full QPaint book: monitor, intake, record, classify, decide, execute, reconcile, set next-review dates, and surface exceptions. James reviews only genuine exceptions via the weekly brief. SURFACE ≠ MANAGE (QPaint commercial decisions) remains unchanged.

## Pipeline Priority

When selecting the next customer, Atlas considers:
- Next Touchpoint Date (overdue > due today)
- Deal Value
- Customer replies (engaged > silent)
- Commercial context (warm > cold > stale)
- Special circumstances (government, committee, insurance)
- Founder Call Day preparation

## Loop Closure (primary learning — 2026-09-01)

SURFACE → RECORD OWNER → SET REVIEW → MONITOR → VERIFY RESPONSE/RESOLUTION → RECONCILE → CLOSE LOOP.

SURFACE ≠ MANAGE does NOT mean "forget the unresolved requirement." Atlas does not own QPaint's decision, but Atlas DOES own visibility of whether the customer requirement remains unresolved. Every item surfaced to QPaint must carry a review date to verify resolution and close the loop. The Arno/Tas miss (surfaced 5 Aug, verified only after 31 Aug) is the canonical failure this prevents.

## Attribution Standard (2026-09-01)

Win Attribution: **Recovered / Assisted / Observed / Pending.**

**Recovered Value = Tier 1 (Recovered) ONLY.** Assisted and Observed wins must have a BLANK Recovered Value. Never populate Recovered Value on an Assisted/Observed win. WON VALUE ≠ ASSISTED VALUE ≠ RECOVERED VALUE.

## Evidence Discipline (2026-09-01)

FACT vs UNKNOWN. "Call outcomes not logged" is FACT. "Calls not made" is UNKNOWN unless James confirms. Never convert missing evidence into a factual claim. State what is proven; leave the rest as UNKNOWN.
