# Decision: DeepSeek Approved for Client Data Processing

**Slug:** `2026-08-17-deepseek-client-data-approved`
**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Status:** Made
**Review Date:** 2026-11-17

---

## The Decision

Not Forgotten Systems is comfortable with DeepSeek processing client data. The AI Provider Boundary established 2026-07-25 (Safety Rule 7 in SOUL.md: "DeepSeek permanently disabled for client data, Gemini-only") is **overturned**. DeepSeek is the approved primary AI provider for all workflows, including those containing client, customer, prospect, quote, email, financial, commercially sensitive, or personal information.

---

## The Problem

The Nightly Dream flagged an anomaly (2026-08-17): `config.yaml` sets DeepSeek (`deepseek-v4-pro`) as the default provider with no Gemini fallback configured. Every QPaint customer email, prospect outreach, and quote was flowing through DeepSeek despite SOUL.md Safety Rule 7 marking it "permanently disabled" for that data. The boundary was documented but never actually enforced in configuration.

The recommendation required a choice: either reconfigure to Gemini (enforcing the boundary), or record an explicit decision that the boundary no longer applies. Josh Hancock confirmed on behalf of the founders: the team is now comfortable with DeepSeek processing client data. The recommendation is therefore invalid.

---

## Options Considered

| # | Option | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| A | Enforce the Gemini-only boundary | Reconfigure `config.yaml` to Gemini primary, restrict DeepSeek to zero-PII system ops. Migration effort, prompt retuning, cost increase. |
| B | Overturn the boundary (chosen) | Accept DeepSeek as the approved primary provider for all data. Update SOUL.md, registers, and documentation to reflect reality. No config change needed. |

---

## Why This Path

The team has evaluated DeepSeek as a provider and is now comfortable with it processing client data. The prior boundary was based on a July 2026 Trust Test assessment (see `knowledge/legal/deepseek-assessment.md`) that failed DeepSeek on all five questions, largely on cross-border jurisdiction and absence of a DPA. That concern has now been consciously accepted by the founders.

Recording the decision (rather than silently letting the drift continue) is the correct move: it removes the contradiction between SOUL.md Safety Rule 7 and the actual operating configuration, and it preserves the reasoning for anyone asking "why do we allow DeepSeek for client data now?" six months later.

---

## Core Principles Applied

- **Trust before growth** — engaged but consciously overridden by founder decision. The founders own the trade-off between provider cost/performance and the cross-border privacy posture.
- **Human accountability** — this is exactly the kind of decision that belongs to the founders, not to Atlas. Atlas recorded it and reconciled all systems; it did not make the call.
- **Security before convenience** — noted as tension. The override means convenience/cost now wins on this specific axis, by explicit founder choice.

## Learnings

1. **A documented boundary that isn't enforced in configuration is not a boundary.** Safety Rule 7 said "enforced by configuration, not instruction" — but the configuration never actually matched the rule. The rule drifted from reality for ~3 weeks before the Nightly Dream caught it. Lesson: when a boundary is declared, the enforcing configuration change must be verified in the same task, not deferred.
2. **Drift-ignoring vs. conscious acceptance.** The correct resolution to a rule/reality mismatch is an explicit decision (this file), not silently editing the rule to match. Recording preserves the "why" for future review.
3. **Founders can override any non-principle rule.** The Six Principles are immutable; Safety Rules are not. This is a clean demonstration of the hierarchy.
