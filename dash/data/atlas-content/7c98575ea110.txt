# Decision: QPaint Relay Authority — relaying an authorised decision is NOT founder-gated

> Relay ≠ decide: once a QPaint decision owner has answered, Atlas communicates it without a founder gate; escalate only to MAKE an unauthorised decision.


**Slug:** `2026-09-24-qpaint-relay-authority`
**Date:** 2026-09-24
**Status:** Made
**Review Date:** 2026-10-24

---

## The Decision

Once an authorised QPaint decision owner has supplied an answer, Atlas has standing authority to COMMUNICATE that answer accurately to the customer (RELATE), RECORD it, RECONCILE it, and MONITOR it — within existing white-label, evidence and safety controls. That communication is NOT founder-gated merely because the answer contains a price, a scope change, a warranty term, a quote-validity date, a scheduling answer, an apology, or another QPaint-authorised commercial fact. Atlas escalates only when it would otherwise need to MAKE an unauthorised decision.

---

## The Problem

Atlas repeatedly escalated routine QPaint work to James under the header "FOUNDER DECISIONS REQUIRED" after the underlying decision had already been made by QPaint. The recurring error was: PRICE / SCOPE / CUSTOMER-FACING → FOUNDER APPROVAL. This conflated three distinct authorities — (1) authority to make a commercial decision, (2) authority to relay an authorised decision, and (3) authority to reconcile an outcome. The result was a decision queue full of items that required zero founder input, violating the "execute, don't re-present" operating standard and stalling customer-service loops (e.g. Ashleigh #43044's darker-stain answer sat unsent awaiting approval).

---

## Options Considered

| # | Option | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| A | Escalate every price/scope/customer-facing item to James (status quo) | Founder becomes routing layer; routine relays stall; violates autonomy directive. |
| B | Relay-authority model: relay/record/reconcile/monitor delegated; escalate only when Atlas must make an unauthorised decision | Atlas executes the customer-service loop; James sees only genuine exceptions. |
| C | Build a new approval system / whitelist / authority database | More governance, more surface for drift; contradicts "do not build more governance." |

---

## Why This Path

Chose B. The underlying authority (qpaint-autonomous-envelope.json) already delegated routine QPaint operations; the failure was interpretive, not a missing permission. Adding more governance (C) would recreate the problem at higher cost. The correction is a clarification of the existing envelope: relaying a decision QPaint already made is execution of the customer-service loop, not a founder decision. This aligns with Principle 6 (human accountability — James owns decisions, Atlas executes) and the CEO Standard (reduce James's workload, surface only genuine exceptions).

---

## Core Principles Applied

- Principle 6 — Human accountability: James owns commercial decisions; Atlas executes the loop.
- Principle 2 — Evidence before opinion: the decision test is driven by "who made the decision," established from primary evidence.
- The CEO Standard — surface genuine exceptions, not a menu of choices.

## Learnings

The escalation trigger is "Atlas would otherwise need to MAKE an unauthorised decision," not "the answer contains a price/scope/warranty/validity fact." Defaults are decisions: "default = no send" is an instruction to not send and monitor, not a founder choice. Authority to send ≠ obligation to send.
