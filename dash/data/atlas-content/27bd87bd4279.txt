## Document 5: Client-Facing Data Processing Disclosure

**Use:** Provided to business clients during onboarding. Explains how their data (and their customers' data) is handled.

---

### How Not Forgotten Handles Your Data

As your quote follow-up partner, we process information about your business and your customers. Here is exactly what happens:

**What we receive from you:**
- Your business name and contact details (at sign-up)
- Quote emails you forward to us (containing your customers' names, contact details, quote scope, and values)

**What we do with it:**
- We extract the information needed to follow up on each quote
- We use AI (Google Gemini, US-based) to help draft professional follow-up messages
- We store your data in Airtable (US) and process emails through Google Workspace
- We track whether follow-up emails are opened so we can report back to you

**What we don't do:**
- We don't sell your data or your customers' data
- We don't use your data for any purpose other than serving you
- We don't share your data with anyone except the services we need to operate (listed above)

**Your obligations:**
- Let your customers know you use a quote follow-up service (we provide suggested wording)
- Forward only quotes that are genuine business opportunities
- Don't forward information that isn't needed for quote follow-up

**Your rights:**
- You can access any data we hold about your business at any time
- You can ask us to correct or delete your data
- You can stop using our service at any time and we will delete your data within 90 days

**Questions:** privacy@notforgotten.com.au
**Full policy:** notforgotten.com.au/privacy

## Document 7: Clauses Requiring Australian Privacy Solicitor Review

The following areas are where professional legal review is recommended before publication. These are not necessarily problems — they are areas where an Australian privacy lawyer's assessment would reduce risk.

| # | Clause / Section | Why Review |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Section 5 — AI Processing (Gemini as primary) | Confirm the Google DPA covers Gemini processing and satisfies APP 8 reasonable steps. Verify the transition from DeepSeek to Gemini is properly documented. |
| **L2** | Section 8 — Cross-Border Disclosure | Review whether the stated "reasonable steps" meet the OAIC's expectations. In particular: are DPAs sufficient without additional contractual clauses? |
| **L3** | Section 2.4 — Clients' Customers (indirect collection) | APP 5 notification for indirectly collected information. Is the framework (client notifies customer + our follow-up footer) sufficient? |
| **L4** | Section 12 — Data Retention | Retention periods stated as commitments. Are any periods inconsistent with legal obligations (e.g., tax record retention, limitation periods for claims)? |
| **L5** | Section 14 — Complaints / OAIC | Confirm OAIC contact details and complaint escalation path are correctly described. |
| **L6** | Entire policy — small business exemption | Confirm whether Not Forgotten qualifies for the small business exemption ($3M turnover threshold) and if so, which APPs still apply. The policy is written as though all APPs apply — this is conservative and good practice, but should be confirmed. |
| **L7** | Entire policy — Notifiable Data Breaches | Confirm NDB scheme applicability. The policy does not describe NDB obligations — this is acceptable because the NDB scheme is operational, not a policy content requirement. But the lawyer should confirm. |
| **L8** | Section 2.3/2.4 — Client vs Customer data | Review the distinction between data we collect as controller (client data) vs data we process as processor (customer data). The policy treats both as "personal information we hold" but the legal obligations may differ. |
| **L9** | Subprocessor list — orgo.ai | Clarify orgo.ai's role — are they a subprocessor (processing personal information) or infrastructure provider (no access to data)? The distinction matters for APP 8 disclosure. |
| **L10** | General — Spam Act compliance | Confirm that the proposed unsubscribe mechanism (reply-to-email) meets the Spam Act's "functional unsubscribe" requirement, particularly for follow-up emails sent on behalf of clients. |
