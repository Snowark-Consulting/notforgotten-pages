# Decision Record: Foundation Complete — Shift to Execution

**Slug:** `2026-08-03-foundation-complete`
**Date:** 2026-08-03
**Status:** Made
**Author:** James Hardstaff

## Context

On 3 August 2026, the foundation phase of Not Forgotten Systems was declared complete. The operating system has been built: Atlas identity, learning framework, trust framework, decision library, experiment methodology, outreach philosophy, log retention standard, storage health policy, and the Headquarters executive operating system.

Two operational policies (Log Retention + Storage Health) and a completed experiment (CvC) demonstrated the system's maturity. The Business Knowledge section was established with three permanent principles.

## Decision

From 4 August 2026 onwards, focus shifts almost entirely to execution.

**What changes:**
- The measure of Atlas is no longer documentation — it is business outcomes
- Success is measured by: recovered revenue, conversations created, customers won, time saved, knowledge accumulated, better decisions made
- Questions shift from "Can we build this?" to "Does it work? Do customers respond? Do conversations start? Do quotes convert? Do clients stay? Does Not Forgotten create measurable value?"

**What stays:**
- The Six Principles remain immutable
- The Trust Test, CEO Standard, Atlas Mandate remain active
- Operational policies (Log Retention, Storage Health) continue running
- The Decision Library and Learning Framework continue accumulating

**James's directive to Atlas:**
- Protect focus. If James drifts back into unnecessary architecture, dashboards, or features that don't materially move the business forward, Atlas must challenge him
- Remind him what we're trying to build
- Help keep attention on highest-value work

## Expected Outcomes

- Customer acquisition becomes the primary metric
- FF1 is the active experiment — every reply is evidence
- QPaint recovery operations continue delivering value
- New client pipeline grows from execution, not architecture
- Atlas's value is measured by business impact, not system completeness
