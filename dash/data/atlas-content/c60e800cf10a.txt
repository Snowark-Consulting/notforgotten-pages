# Decision: Industry Fluency Over Company Research

**Slug:** `2026-07-03-industry-fluency-over-company-research`
**Date:** 2026-07-03
**Status:** Made
**Review Date:** 2026-09-03

---

## The Decision

Outreach personalisation shifts from company-level research (website facts, services, certifications) to industry-level operational fluency (understanding business models, quoting dynamics, structural tensions). Every email must demonstrate that we understand how their industry operates — not that we scraped their website. Company-specific details are seasoning, not the meal.

---

## The Problem

The current personalisation approach (Principle 2: "Personalise with genuine research") had Atlas scraping websites for facts: services offered, client lists, locations, certifications. This produced emails that sounded researched rather than insightful. The prospect thought "this person read my website" rather than "this person understands my business."

James identified that surface-level company personalisation doesn't build trust — it signals effort without insight. The emails that work create a feeling of being understood at the operational level, not the factual level.

---

## Options Considered

| # | Option | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Keep company-first research** — continue as-is, refine the execution | No fundamental change. Risk: emails continue to sound researched rather than insightful |
| B | **Industry fluency as primary, company as secondary** — identify industry quoting dynamics first, use company facts only to sharpen operational observations | Structural shift in how we prepare for outreach. Requires new framework, reference docs, and skill updates |
| C | **Pure industry, no company research** — drop company-specific details entirely | Too generic. Would produce emails that could be sent to anyone in the industry |

---

## Why This Path

Chose Option B. The core insight: a business owner feels understood when you name the operational realities of their industry — not when you list facts from their About page. "Commercial cleaning runs on two very different engines: recurring contracts and one-off jobs" lands differently than "ACS covers medical, dental, schools, offices, strata."

Company-specific details still have value — but only when they sharpen an operational observation. The 5-question industry-quoting-dynamics framework ensures observations come from first principles, not website copy.

---

## Core Principles Applied

- Principle 7 (Outreach Philosophy): "The sequence: relationship → questions → recognition → proof → solution" — industry fluency accelerates the recognition phase
- New Principle 9: "Demonstrate industry fluency, not company research"
- Evidence before optimisation: this is a hypothesis grounded in pattern recognition from 15 sent / 0 replies. The campaign data will validate or refute it
- "Help prospects discover their own problem" (Founder Interview): industry observations help them see the structural gap they live with daily

---

## Risks Identified

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Industry observations feel generic without company specifics | Med | Med | The industry-quoting-dynamics framework teaches how to make observations specific through operational insight, not facts. Test-and-learn. |
| Different businesses in same industry get similar emails | Low | Low | Company-specific seasoning plus genuine question variation prevents cookie-cutter feel. |
| Industry fluency harder for Atlas to generate than website scraping | Med | Med | The 5-question framework + worked examples provide the structure. Atlas's training knowledge of business models is deeper than its knowledge of individual companies. |

---

## Expected Outcome

Emails shift from "this person researched me" to "this person understands my world." Reply rates should improve because prospects feel genuinely understood rather than targeted. The industry-fluent approach also scales better — one industry analysis serves an entire campaign rather than per-company research.

---

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Reply rate improvement over calibration baseline (0/15) within first campaign using industry-fluent approach
- [ ] Qualitative signal: prospects who reply mention feeling understood rather than researched
- [ ] Atlas can independently produce industry-fluent observations using the 5-question framework without defaulting to website facts
- [ ] James approves drafts with fewer "too researched" corrections
