## The decision being reviewed

Adopted 28 Jun 2026, as the operating baseline for all outreach. Three meta-principles:

1. The strategy is a **living system**, not a fixed playbook — when data contradicts assumptions we
   challenge our own thinking and adapt.
2. **Ending a campaign does not end a relationship** — a sequence may stop at 4 emails, but genuine
   reconnection months later should be recognised and acted on.
3. **Context-awareness overrides timetables** — if a business, industry or circumstance changes, adapt
   rather than follow a schedule.

The decision shipped five success criteria and four expected outcomes. This prep scores both.

### The honest finding

**The living system has adapted repeatedly and successfully. It has not yet improved the outcome it
exists to improve.** Four strategy changes in 90 days is a working adaptation loop — the decision's
mechanism is proven. But adaptation has not moved the acquisition number: 1 client, 1.9% reply rate, and
the channel now quiet for 17 days. That is not a failure of the living-system principle; it is the
principle operating exactly as designed, on evidence that keeps saying the same thing.

**Confidence: High (90%)** for the fact pattern (all figures verified against on-file reports and a live
Airtable pull tonight). **Confidence: Medium (75%)** for the causal reading — "messaging is the binding
constraint" is one hypothesis; the alternative is that cold email to owner-led painters is a structurally
weak acquisition channel regardless of message, and the 30 Aug review's own conclusion (founder calls,
not email) points at the second.

### The risk that fired

The decision listed "living system becomes *no system* — adaptability becomes an excuse for
inconsistency" at **Med likelihood / High impact**. The 90-day evidence says this risk partially
materialised, in a specific and diagnosable way: **the living system has no exit state.** When the
evidence said "stop", the system held (correctly) but had nowhere to put the held records — so 52
prospects have read as "due" on every scan since 4 Sep, and the channel went dormant without being
formally closed. The system can decide to stop. It cannot yet record that it has stopped.

This is the same defect surfaced from two independent directions — the V2 review prep (due today,
`2026-09-21-v2-email2-observation-review-prep.md`, verdict question 3) and here. Two independent
derivations of one gap is the strongest signal in this prep.
**Confidence: High (88%)** — the 52 records and the missing exit state are both directly verified.

## Verdict questions for James

1. **Is the living-system principle itself affirmed?** The evidence says the adaptation mechanism works
   (4 documented data-driven changes in 90 days). Nothing found argues for reverting to a fixed playbook.
2. **Does "living system" require a defined exit state to be safe at scale?** The one risk the decision
   flagged is the one that fired. A MONITOR state — clear the follow-up date, append a dated note,
   stage untouched, reversible, no contact — would close it. Recommended mechanism is already specified
   and already blocked on a founder ruling (identical to verdict question 3 of the V2 review prep).
3. **Is success measured by system improvement or acquisition improvement?** Criterion 3 passed on
   internal changes; the acquisition number is 1 client. If the standard is the latter, the 28 Sep review
   should say so explicitly, because it changes what the next 90 days optimise.
4. **Does criterion 2 (per-industry pattern analysis) need a decision, or a portfolio?** It could not be
   met because outreach has been single-vertical, not because analysis was skipped. Either it is reframed
   as vertical-depth (met, and met well), or a second vertical must be opened for it to be testable.
5. **Should the Travis-led Email 1 pilot, still awaiting approval since August, be resolved at this
   review?** It is the one recommendation from the July intelligence report with no verdict, and it is
   the direct test of the only conversion trigger ever observed.
