## 5. What evidence would cause me to change the current outreach philosophy?

### Evidence that would strengthen current approach:
- Any reply that engages with the question itself (not asking "what do you sell?")
- A reply rate of 33%+ across the full 10-prospect calibration campaign
- Replies that reference the specific observation we made (showing the research landed)
- Any reply that names their own follow-up gap unprompted

### Evidence that would trigger a philosophy review:

| Observation | What it would challenge | Proposed response |
|---|---|---|
| 0/3 replies within 14 days | "One observation, one question, one reason" isn't enough to earn a reply from agencies | Test adding a relevance statement ("I'm asking because...") that is more direct about why this matters to their revenue |
| All replies ask "what do you sell?" or "who are you?" | The curiosity gap isn't working — they're confused, not curious | The question needs to more clearly bridge to the problem we solve without naming the product |
| Replies all ignore the question and go straight to product inquiry | The question isn't interesting enough to engage with on its own terms | Re-evaluate question quality — it should be genuinely thought-provoking, not just a polite lead-in |
| 0% reply rate from business-name fallback prospects vs >0% from named-contact prospects | The "Hi Business Name" greeting rule may suppress replies | Invest in contact name research before sending; test whether a named contact changes response rates |
| >50% reply rate across full campaign | The approach is stronger than expected | Tighten the sequence (fewer touches, shorter intervals); we may be under-asking |
| Reply quality varies significantly by industry | Industry-specific hooks matter more than the generic question framework | Invest in deeper industry research before scaling; customise the question per vertical |

### Evidence that would change foundational assumptions:
- If a reply explicitly says "this felt like a sales email": the entire "don't pitch" framing isn't being perceived as intended
- If 0 replies from any agency but >0 from painters/cleaners: agencies may need a fundamentally different approach — the question framework may only work for trades/service businesses where follow-up is universally neglected
- If a reply comes back angry/annoyed: we misjudged the tone or the recipient's tolerance for unsolicited email

## Meta-Note

These predictions are recorded before any replies have arrived. The purpose is not to be right — it's to have a documented baseline that I can compare against reality and learn from. If my predictions are wrong, the gap between prediction and outcome is the most valuable data this campaign can produce.

The specific things I want to learn:
1. Does business size predict reply likelihood (or does it invert — bigger = harder to reach the right person)?
2. Does a more specific observation (OMG's scale) produce a higher reply rate than a generic one (CCM's services)?
3. Do Melbourne businesses reply faster than Sydney businesses?
4. Does hello@ vs enquiries@ vs service@ affect delivery or reply rates?
