## 2. What We Learned

### Patterns Observed

**Zero replies is itself a pattern.** This is the most significant finding of the review. Across 30 carefully crafted, industry-specific emails sent to 10 businesses across 5 industries, not a single prospect replied. This is not a small-sample fluctuation — it's an unambiguous signal.

**Possible interpretations (ranked by likelihood):**

1. **The cold-email approach doesn't work for these industries (IT MSP, Construction, B2B Services, Engineering, Cleaning) at this stage of NF's maturity.** These are not tradies. Their quoting processes are more complex, their decision-makers are harder to reach, and their inboxes are more competitive. The Travis Krieg case study, while compelling, may not bridge the credibility gap for non-trades prospects.

2. **The emails are landing in spam or promotions tabs.** Without open-rate data and delivery monitoring beyond bounce detection, this cannot be ruled out.

3. **The sample is too small to draw industry-level conclusions, but large enough to flag a fundamental approach issue.** 0/30 is statistically significant regardless of sample size.

4. **The contact information is wrong or outdated.** The contact names for 9 of 10 prospects were unknown (business name greeting used). Emails went to generic addresses (info@, hello@, enquiries@, sales@, admin@). These may not reach decision-makers.

### Objections Received

None. Zero replies means zero objections to categorise. This itself is telling — we're not even getting "not interested."

### Unexpected Results

**Complete silence was not predicted.** The pre-wave hypothesis, based on calibration campaign patterns, was that industry-fluency personalisation + curiosity-led framing would generate at least a few T2 ("not for us but thanks") or T3 ("tell me more") replies across 10 prospects. Zero is below any reasonable expectation.

**The "different industry, same problem" framing didn't land.** Email 3 explicitly acknowledged the industry gap ("Different industry entirely," "I know it's a different universe") while arguing the pattern underneath was identical. This approach was designed to bridge the Travis Krieg case study to non-trades verticals. It generated no replies. The framing may have backfired — by highlighting the gap, we may have reinforced it.

**Email 2 questions got no responses either.** Email 2 asked genuine operational questions ("Who ends up wearing that?", "How do you balance...?", "Do you adjust by industry?"). These were designed to be easy to reply to. The fact that zero prospects engaged with these questions suggests the email never reached a person who cared, or the sender wasn't trusted enough to warrant a reply.

## 3. What Should Change

### Messaging

| Change | Evidence | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Test significantly shorter Email 1 (40-60 words).** Current drafts are 70-120 words. If emails aren't being read, length is a barrier. Shorter emails may not improve reply rates but will produce cleaner data — if short emails also get 0 replies, the problem is landing/deliverability, not content. | 0 replies across 30 long-form emails | **High** |
| **Test proof-led Email 1 for non-trades.** The Travis case study was deferred to Email 3. An earlier deployment (Email 1 or 2) might build credibility faster, especially for industries where a painting case study feels distant. | Email 3 Travis deployment generated 0 replies | **Medium** |
| **Consider dropping the "different industry, same problem" framing.** By explicitly acknowledging the gap between Travis and the prospect's industry, we may be reinforcing the disconnect rather than bridging it. | Email 3 framing generated 0 replies; no replies to any touchpoint | **Medium** |

### Subject Lines

| Change | Evidence | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Test question-led and very-short subjects for Email 1.** Current Email 1 subjects are observation-led and 3-6 words. A question like "A Question About Your Proposals" may generate more opens. Without open-rate data, this is speculative — but worth testing given the 0% reply rate. | 0 replies across all Email 1 subjects | **Medium** |
| **Test removing per-prospect unique subjects.** Uniform subjects ("one more question", "a real example") performed identically to unique industry subjects — both got 0 replies. The overhead of per-prospect subject research may not be justified. | Equal performance (0 vs 0) | **Low** |

### Personalisation

| Change | Evidence | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Test company-specific personalisation (not just industry-fluency).** Current Email 1s demonstrate industry understanding but not company-specific insight. A single specific detail about their business might increase trust and open rates. This contradicts Principle 2 (personalise with industry fluency, not company research) — but the principle itself may need revision based on this wave's evidence. | 0 replies from industry-fluency-only personalisation | **High** |
| **Prioritise finding contact names.** 9 of 10 prospects were addressed by business name. This is within the cold-email skill rules but may reduce reply rates. The calibration campaign had contact names for 0 of 10 prospects — this should be tracked as a variable. | 90% of emails used business-name greeting | **Medium** |

### ICP Assumptions

| Change | Evidence | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Reconsider whether IT MSP, Construction, B2B Services, Engineering belong in ICP at current stage.** These industries generated 0 replies from 8 prospects (all except Cleaning). Their quoting processes are complex, their decision-makers are hard to reach via generic email addresses, and the Travis case study may not resonate. Focus on trades where the product and proof are strongest. | 0/8 non-trades replies | **High** |
| **Test whether smaller businesses (<20 employees) respond differently.** The 10 prospects span from small MSPs to large construction firms. Size might be a confounding variable. | Insufficient data — need to isolate | **Medium** |

### Follow-Up Sequence

| Change | Evidence | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Keep the 4-email sequence with breakup.** Don't shorten to 3 or extend to 5 without evidence that 4 is wrong. Email 4 may yet generate replies. | Email 4 not yet sent; can't assess sequence length | **Low** |
| **Add more value in Email 2 before going to case study.** Email 2 asked another question — which is consistent with the "two questions before proof" pattern. But a tool, calculator, or useful resource might perform better than a second question. | 0 replies to Email 2 questions | **Medium** |

### Timing

| Change | Evidence | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Never send on weekends.** This is already policy (Weekend Send Prohibition, Jul 12). The Sunday Jul 12 batch was a timing deviation — protocol was followed (no recall). Enforce more strictly. | Email 3 sent Sunday; 0 replies; cannot isolate timing effect | **High** |
| **Shorten Email 1→2 gap to 5 days (from 7).** The standard cadence is +5/+3/+7. Wave 1 used +7/+3/+8. Align with standard unless evidence supports a deviation. | Standard cadence defined in cold-email skill but not followed | **Low** |

## 6. What to Test Next

| Experiment | Hypothesis | Variable Isolated | How Measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trades vs non-trades split test.** Send the same Email 1 structure to 10 painters and 10 non-trades. | Painters will reply at higher rates than non-trades because the product and case study are directly relevant. | Prospect industry | Reply rate per cohort |
| **Ultra-short Email 1 (40-60 words).** Cut Email 1 to observation + question only. Remove the "why I'm asking" sentence. | Shorter emails will generate more replies because they respect the reader's time and feel less like a campaign. | Email length | Reply rate vs 70-120 word baseline |
| **Company-specific hook in Email 1.** Add one specific detail about their business (a recent project, a visible process gap, an operational signal from their website). | Company-specific personalisation will increase trust and reply rates compared to industry-fluency alone. | Personalisation depth | Reply rate vs industry-fluency-only baseline |
| **Contact-name-only outreach.** Send only to prospects where a contact name is confirmed (not business-name greeting). | Named recipients will reply at higher rates than business-name greetings. | Greeting type | Reply rate per greeting type |
| **Tool-led Email 1.** Give away a calculator or diagnostic tool. No product mention, no Travis case study. | A genuinely useful tool generates curiosity and replies better than an observation + question email. | Email format | Reply rate vs observation-led baseline |
