# QPaint Customer Send Incident — 2026-08-19

**Status:** OPEN — full stop in effect. No QPaint customer communication authorised.
**Reporter:** James Hardstaff (Founder)
**Incident owner:** Atlas (self-audit)

---

## 1. Summary

6 generic QPaint Email 1 ("recovery-introduction") emails were sent to real QPaint customers.
The emails were mechanically correct (right recipient, right sender, right signature, right
footer, right template checksum, no duplicate, no reply, no suppression) but **contextually
empty**: each customer's actual quote, project, timing and decision stage were not understood
before contacting them. The template was applied as mail-merge (name + interior/exterior)
instead of being a framework applied only after the individual customer was understood.

**Total emails sent:** 6
**Total customers affected:** 6
**No Email 2 / Email 3 / SMS / calls sent.** Two Email 2 sends (Beth McIntosh, Julius Gloria)
were pending but NOT sent — the stop arrived first.

## 4. Classification (A / B / C / D)

- **A (appropriate despite generic):** none
- **B (suboptimal, unlikely to confuse individually):** Ron Hard, Sue Barrett, Georgina Martin,
  Sara von Nordheim, Raewyn Frost
- **C (inappropriate given known context):** Adrian Cook — scope is "clear timber finish"
  (Intergrain Heavy Duty), not paint; describing it as "exterior painting" is a mild
  mischaracterisation of what he was actually quoted for.
- **D (material customer/reputation risk):** none individually.

**Collective position:** the 6 emails are individually B (with one C) — factually accurate at
the interior/exterior level, correctly branded, non-pushy, compliant footer — but the systemic
failure (sending generic mail-merge to 6 real customers without understanding each one) is a
genuine **QPaint reputation risk**, which is why this is treated as serious.

---

## 5. Potential customer harm / QPaint reputation risk

- Fresh-intake customers (quote received 2–3 days prior) were contacted by a third party
  (Atlas) with a generic "have you received your quote" message. Risk: confusion about who
  Atlas is and why a third party is contacting them about a very recent QPaint quote.
- Generic wording does not reflect the individual project (lead-paint test, clear-timber
  finish, optional extras, peeling ceilings), so it does not demonstrate that we understand
  their job — undermining the trust QPaint entrusts to us.

---

## 6. Protocol expected vs actually executed

**Expected:** understand the individual customer (who / what quoted / when / history / decision
stage / correct next action) BEFORE contacting them; use the canonical template as a framework;
HOLD if the template doesn't fit or evidence is insufficient.

**Actually executed:** verified mechanical/technical correctness (recipient, sender, signature,
footer, template checksum, no duplicate, no reply, no suppression, interior-vs-exterior), then
sent identical template copy with name + scope substituted. No per-customer context assembly,
no decision-stage assessment, no context-appropriateness gate.

---

## 7. Root cause

**Primary (Atlas):** I reduced "customer communication" to "template execution." I treated
"use the canonical template, do not modify copy" as "send identical generic copy with variables
substituted," which is exactly the misreading flagged. I did not assemble each customer's
project/timing/history and did not ask whether the email made sense against that customer's
complete history.

**Contributing:**
1. **Customer-context assembly was shallow** — rich, customer-specific scope data (specific
   areas, finishes, options, lead-paint test) and intake history were available in Airtable but
   were reduced to "interior"/"exterior".
2. **No Customer Context Gate existed** — Voice Review (tone/clichés/coffee test), Technical
   Verification (em-dash, placeholder, footer, branding) and canonical_send's 10 gates
   (delivery/duplicate/reply/suppression/state) all passed a generic, context-free email.
3. **I over-applied "do not modify copy"** — treated the template as the whole answer rather
   than a framework to apply only after the customer is understood (else HOLD).
4. **Fresh-intake context not weighed** — these were 2–3-day-old quotes, not aged recovery
   quotes; I did not consider whether the "recovery-introduction" framing fit that timing.

**What did NOT fail (for accuracy):** recipient identity, duplicate/reply/suppression checks,
sender identity, signature/footer compliance, template checksum integrity. The failure is
specifically in the customer-understanding/context layer, not the mechanical/compliance layer.

**Failure location:** customer-context assembly + personalisation + gateway validation
(no context gate) + my over-literal reading of the template instruction. Combination.

---

## 8. Why the existing gateway passed

The existing gateways only test mechanical/technical correctness:
- Voice Review (9-point): tone, no clichés, no exaggeration, coffee test — a generic
  introduction passes all of these.
- Technical Verification: em-dash scan, placeholder scan, footer-exactly-once, NF-branding
  only-in-footer, no value leak, correct greeting/sign-off — a generic mail-merge passes all.
- canonical_send 10 gates: classification, idempotency, founder-approval artefact, Gmail
  reply/duplicate, send, Sent-verify, Messages, evidence, state-transition, read-back,
  reconciliation — none ask "is this contextually appropriate for THIS customer?"

There is no gate that answers: "Given everything we know about this customer, is this the
correct next action and is every material sentence appropriate?" — the missing
**Customer Context Gate**.

## 12. Proposed corrective protocol (for Founder decision — NOT yet implemented)

1. **Customer Context Gate (mandatory, before any send).** For each customer, assemble and
   document: who / what quoted / when / history / QPaint prior comms / customer prior comms /
   decision stage / correct next action / why contact today / what this email says to this
   specific person. If any of these cannot be answered from evidence → HOLD.
2. **Template = framework, not permission.** If the canonical template does not cleanly fit a
   customer's circumstances, HOLD or produce a customer-specific draft under the approved
   protocol. Silence is not permission to send generic copy.
3. **Personalisation ≠ mail merge.** Only verified, customer-specific facts may personalise an
   email. No invented personalisation.
4. **Fresh-intake vs aged-recovery distinction** enforced at template selection time.
