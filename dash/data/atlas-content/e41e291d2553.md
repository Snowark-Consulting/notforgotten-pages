# Founding Decision: Travis Case Study as Bridge, Not Opener

## The Decision

The Travis Krieg Painting case study ($177,555 recovered, 14 jobs, 5-person crew) is Not Forgotten's most powerful proof point — but it must not be deployed in the first contact with a prospect. It belongs after the prospect has recognised their own follow-up gap, as a bridge between problem recognition and confidence to act. Lead with the case study and you're telling someone else's story. Lead with questions and the prospect tells their own.

---

## The Problem

Every startup wants to lead with its best proof. Not Forgotten has a genuinely impressive one: a real Adelaide painting business recovered $177,555 through systematic follow-up. The temptation is to open every conversation with some version of "we helped a painter recover $177K — want to know how?"

The problem: proof without relevance doesn't create belief. A commercial cleaner reading about a painter's success might think "that's impressive, but my business is different." A marketing agency owner thinks "trades are not my world." The case study becomes noise, not signal.

James's insight from the interview: "People buy when they recognise themselves in the story, not because someone else succeeded." The Travis story only becomes powerful after the prospect has said — or thought — "that sounds like my situation."

---

## Options Considered

| # | Option | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| A | Lead with Travis immediately | Open every email with the case study. "We helped a painter recover $177K." Risk: impressive but irrelevant to many. |
| B | Never mention Travis externally | Keep the case study for sales calls only. Risk: wastes the strongest proof we have. |
| C | Travis as bridge, not opener | Deploy after problem recognition, before solution pitch. Sequence: question → gap recognition → Travis proof → solution. |
| D | Travis as closer only | Use only when prospect is already interested but hesitant. Risk: delays proof too long, loses momentum. |

---

## Why This Path

Option C was chosen because it respects the psychology of belief. People don't buy because someone else succeeded. They buy because they believe THEY can succeed. The Travis case study answers the question "does this actually work?" — but that question only matters after the prospect has asked themselves "do I have this problem?"

The sequence: (1) Ask a question that surfaces their follow-up gap → (2) Prospect recognises "I don't actually know what happens to my quotes" → (3) "Here's what happened when a painter in Adelaide got serious about this: $177,555 recovered" → (4) "Would you like to see if something similar is possible for your business?"

Step 3 only works because steps 1 and 2 happened first. Without them, it's a brag. With them, it's evidence.

---

## Core Principles Applied

- **Help prospects discover their own problem** — The case study validates a problem the prospect has already recognised. It doesn't create the recognition. (Source: Philosophical Directive 2)
- **The Travis case study is a bridge, not an opening line** — This is literally the directive. It's rare that a principle is this explicit. (Source: Philosophical Directive 3)
- **Certainty, not features** — The case study doesn't say "our software does X." It says "this business stopped losing opportunities." The outcome is the message. (Source: Philosophical Directive 1)

## Risks Identified

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delaying proof costs credibility — prospects want evidence early | Medium | Medium | The question itself is a form of credibility. "I'm asking because I've seen this work" is embedded in the approach. If prospects explicitly ask for proof, deploy Travis immediately. |
| Travis is a painter — prospects in other verticals may not relate | Medium | Medium | Frame Travis around the universal problem (lost follow-up) not the specific trade (painting). "An Adelaide service business" not "a painter." Develop case studies in other verticals as soon as possible. |
| Competitors with case studies lead with them and capture attention | Low | Low | If a competitor is winning with a different approach, test. But the principle is based on how belief works, not competitive positioning. |
| Sales calls without Travis upfront feel unsubstantiated | Low | Medium | On calls, deploy Travis earlier than in email. The medium is different — a conversation can establish relevance faster than text. |

## Expected Outcome

Prospects who reach the Travis case study after self-recognising their gap convert at a higher rate than if shown Travis upfront. The case study becomes a validation tool, not a cold opener. Across the first 50 prospects who enter conversation, the Travis bridge sequence performs measurably better than any alternative sequence.

---

## Success Criteria

- [ ] At least 70% of prospects who enter active conversation are shown Travis only after they've articulated their own follow-up gap
- [ ] Conversion rate from Travis-shown to demo-booked is higher than industry SaaS benchmark (typically 10-15%)
- [ ] At least 2 prospects cite the Travis case study as influential in their decision to try Not Forgotten
- [ ] No prospect reports feeling "pitched" or "sold to" when Travis is introduced (it should feel like natural evidence, not a hard sell)
- [ ] At least one case study developed in a second vertical (cleaning or agency) within 6 months

## Atlas's Challenge (Pre-Decision)

> This decision was shaped during the interview when James explicitly articulated the reasoning. The challenge wasn't about whether the principle is right — it's clearly correct — but about edge cases. If a prospect from the painting industry is contacted, does the rule still apply? Probably not — a painter seeing another painter's results is immediate relevance. The principle should be: Travis is a bridge for non-painters; for painters, it might be an opener. James agreed this nuance is worth tracking but the general rule stands.

**James's response:** Principle stands. For painting prospects specifically, Travis can appear earlier in the sequence. For all other verticals, bridge-only.
