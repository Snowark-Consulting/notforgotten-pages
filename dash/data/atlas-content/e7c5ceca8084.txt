# Relationship-Aware Follow-Up + Government/QBuild Handling — 2026-08-21

## 1. Relationship-aware follow-up (product requirement)

Multiple Airtable quote records belonging to the same human/business must be recognised as ONE
relationship, not independent customer journeys. Before any customer outbound, Atlas must check
whether that person has: multiple active quotes, previous quotes, previous replies/conversations,
Won work with QPaint, Lost work with QPaint, an existing relationship with James/QPaint, or
another active NF communication thread.

**Evidence:** Dean Baker (3 QBuild quotes to one contact), Nicholas Zappacosta (Won Logan Village +
open Beenleigh quote).

**Status:** Requirement identified. No infrastructure/Airtable redesign/automation authorised yet.

## 2. Government / QBuild = QPaint-managed, not customer follow-up

QBuild/government work arrives as "QBUILD PAINTING WORKS … As per QBuild specifications" with
90-day validity and school-holiday timing. These are formal tender/procurement submissions, NOT
customer quotes requiring follow-up. QPaint manages the QBuild relationship directly
(James ↔ Tas ↔ QBuild). NF's role is tender facilitation/observation, not Email 1 follow-up.

A "have you received your quote, any questions?" email to a QBuild procurement officer would be
inappropriate and damage QPaint's credibility.

**Evidence:** Logan Village SS RFT (Won), Nicholas Beenleigh Ambulance Station (HOLD), Dean Baker
3 quotes (Caboolture/Kallangur/Tullawong — QBuild tender).

## 3. Customer history review must include BOTH mailboxes

Vivien Lennan (14 Aug 2026): Atlas sent Email 2 while Vivien was already in a James (james@)
conversation. She replied "responded to James yesterday." Root cause: communication-history review
covered atlas@ only, not james@.

A customer actively speaking with James must not receive an unrelated Atlas follow-up simply
because Airtable says a touch is due. This belongs inside **CUSTOMER HISTORY REVIEW**
(the `communication_history_reviewed` step of the Customer Context Gate), not a standalone workflow.
