## Experiment Design

| Variable | Control (A) — 10 prospects | Test (B) — 10 prospects |
|---|---|---|
| **Approach** | Industry-fluent observation → curiosity question → why asking | Direct question → minimal frame → why asking |
| **Length** | 70-120 words | 40-60 words |
| **Philosophy** | Demonstrates understanding before asking | Leads with the question, context is minimal |
| **Hypothesis** | If we demonstrate industry understanding first, prospects feel understood and reply | If we reduce friction and get straight to the point, busy owners are more likely to reply |

**Prediction:** Both variants will outperform the 0% reply rate from Wave 1 calibration (different vertical mix, different timing). Test (B) may produce higher reply volume; Control (A) may produce higher reply quality. Confidence: Low — first controlled A/B test.

**Single variable changed:** Email body framing and length. Subject line, sender, timing, CTA, and all principles remain identical.

## 4-Email Sequence

### Cadence (Proposal 2 approved — shortened from 5-3-7 to 3-3-7)

|---|---|---|
| Email 1 | Day 0 | Mon Jul 13 |
| Email 2 | Day +3 | Thu Jul 16 |

| Email 4 | Day +13 | Sun Jul 26 → Mon Jul 27 (weekend push) |

### Email 1 — First Contact (both variants)

**Subject:** Your Painting Quotes

**Control (A):**
```
Hi [Name],

[Observation grounded in how painting businesses operate — quoting rhythm, time cost, the gap between quote and follow-up. Specific to this prospect's geography or niche where possible.]

[One genuine question that bridges to follow-up — makes them think about what happens after a quote goes out. Not leading. Not rhetorical.]

[One sentence on why asking — honest, no pitch.]
```

**Test (B):**
```
Hi [Name],

[One direct question about how they handle quote follow-up. Gets straight to the point. No preamble observation.]

[One sentence of context — why asking, honest and brief.]
```

### Email 2 — Second Touch (Day +3, both variants)

**Subject:** Re: Your Painting Quotes

Adds a new observation or angle. Does not reference Email 1 as if they read it. No false engagement. New value: a different aspect of the quoting problem (e.g., time cost of quoting vs. painting, or the emotional weight of silent quotes).

**Control (A):** New industry observation, different angle. ~70-100 words.
**Test (B):** Second question, different angle. ~40-50 words.

### Email 3 — Travis Bridge (Day +6, both variants)

**Subject:** A painter in Adelaide recovered $177K

First introduction of social proof. No pitch — just the Travis Krieg story as a bridge. "This painter had the same problem. Here's what happened. Does any of this resonate?"

**Control (A):** Travis story with context about why it matters. ~100-120 words.
**Test (B):** Travis story, shorter. ~60-80 words.

### Email 4 — Breakup (Day +13, both variants)

**Subject:** Last one from me

Leave the door open. No pressure. "If this isn't the right time, no worries." Both variants use the same breakup template. ~50-70 words.

## Industry Quoting Dynamics — Painters

- **Business model:** Project-based. Residential (homeowners) + commercial (builders, property managers). Mix varies by business.
- **Quoting rhythm:** Quotes done evenings/weekends between jobs. Time quoting = time not painting = time not earning.
- **Quote volume:** 5-30 quotes per week depending on business size. Higher in spring/summer.
- **Structural tension:** Owner does the quoting AND the painting. Follow-up gets deprioritised because there's always another job to start, another quote to write, another client to meet.
- **After a quote goes out:** Usually silence. "Just checking in" text or call after a week or two. No system. Memory and hope.
- **Why they'd feel the gap:** Every silent quote is time invested with no return. The money is already half-won — they did the work of quoting. A painter who sends 15 quotes a week at $3K average is letting $45K in potential revenue sit in other people's inboxes.
