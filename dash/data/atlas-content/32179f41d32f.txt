# Decision: Diagnose-Before-Change + CEO Briefing Scope Refinement

> Two operational refinements: diagnose before major change + routine outreach executed, not reviewed.

**Slug:** `2026-07-13-diagnose-before-change-ceo-briefing-scope`
**Date:** 2026-07-13
**Status:** Made
**Review Date:** 2026-09-13

---

## The Decision

Two operational refinements:

1. **Diagnose before recommending major change.** When a campaign underperforms, work through the evidence chain (deliverability → opens → clicks → replies), identify the most likely bottleneck, then recommend the smallest experiment most likely to improve it. No large strategic changes when a smaller, evidence-based experiment could answer the question.

2. **Operate within delegated authority — routine outreach is executed, not reviewed.** Atlas does not present routine outreach drafts to James for review. Full stop. The workflow is: draft → Voice Review → Technical Verification → send via `send-outreach.py` → reconcile Airtable → report in CEO Briefing. James only sees an outreach draft before sending if: (a) Atlas is genuinely uncertain, (b) there's a factual error or trust-damaging issue, (c) the email falls outside an approved sequence, or (d) the email requires a strategic decision outside delegated authority. Otherwise: execute. The CEO Briefing tells James what was executed, what happened, what was learned, and what strategic decisions (if any) require his input. If a strategic change is needed, Atlas presents: evidence, diagnosis, smallest experiment recommended, expected outcome and trade-offs. James decides the strategy. Atlas executes it.

---

## The Problem

Two issues had emerged:

1. **Campaign underperformance → instinct to recommend fundamental revision.** Without a structured diagnostic framework, there was a risk of jumping past small, testable hypotheses to larger strategic changes. This violated the principle of evidence before optimisation.

2. **CEO Briefing was presenting routine execution work as "awaiting approval."** After the Jul 12 Tier 1 delegation decision, the briefing format still included routine email drafts as decisions for James. This cluttered the briefing and undermined the delegation boundary. James wants the briefing to be a strategic decision surface, not an execution queue.

---

## Options Considered

| # | Option | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| A | Full delegation — no CEO review of any outreach, ever | Too aggressive. Removes James from strategic oversight of campaigns. |
| B | Keep current Tier 1/Tier 2 split, refine briefing format | The Jul 12 delegation was right in principle but the briefing hadn't caught up. Refine the briefing filter. |
| C | Reverse delegation — back to all-draft review | Regressive. Slower, doesn't build Atlas capability. |

**Chose B** with a sharper filter: diagnostic framework for recommendations + "Chief of Staff test" for briefing items.

---

## Why This Path

- Option A removes James from strategic visibility entirely — he still needs to see campaign performance and approve pivots.
- Option C reverses the trust built. James explicitly said "Routine execution is now your responsibility."
- Option B sharpens the existing delegation without changing its architecture. The briefing becomes a CEO decision surface. The diagnostic framework ensures recommendations are evidence-grounded.

Trade-off accepted: Atlas might occasionally send an email James would have tweaked. That's the cost of delegation. The Campaign Review catches patterns.

---

## Core Principles Applied

- **Evidence before optimisation** — the diagnostic framework enforces the evidence chain before any recommendation
- **Tiered trust** (from `founding-tiered-trust-automation`) — trust expands with demonstrated reliability
- **James's role is strategic decisions; Atlas's role is execution** — the "Chief of Staff test" formalises this boundary
- **Learn as quickly as possible** — the diagnostic framework accelerates learning by isolating variables

---

## Risks Identified

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atlas sends an email below standard | Low | Low | Voice Review + Technical Verification gates. Campaign Review catches patterns. |
| Strategic decision slips through without James | Low | Med | "Chief of Staff test" as pre-briefing filter. In doubt, escalate. |
| Diagnostic framework applied too rigidly | Low | Low | Framework is guide, not dogma. Complex cases may need broader investigation. |

---

## Expected Outcome

- CEO Briefing becomes a strategic decision surface, not an execution queue
- Routine outreach executes faster (no briefing bottleneck)
- Campaign recommendations are evidence-grounded, starting from the smallest testable hypothesis
- Atlas develops independent judgment about what requires CEO attention vs. what falls within delegation

---

## Success Criteria

- [ ] CEO Briefing contains zero routine "approve this draft" items after Jul 20
- [ ] Next campaign underperformance → recommendation follows evidence chain (deliverability → opens → clicks → replies)
- [ ] No campaign pivots proposed without first diagnosing the bottleneck
- [ ] James does not have to redirect briefing items back to Atlas ("this is your call")

## Atlas's Challenge (Pre-Decision)

No challenge. These refinements directly strengthen the Jul 12 delegation decision and align with the founding principle of tiered trust. The diagnostic framework is a natural extension of the Learning Framework. The only watch point: the "Chief of Staff test" requires judgment — what's strategic vs. executional can blur. If in doubt, escalate briefly with rationale.
