## The Decision

Adopted a 7-stage prospect lifecycle built from first principles: **New → Outreach Active → Engaged → Qualified Opportunity → Won / Campaign Complete / Lost**. Observable transitions (email sent, reply received, sequence completed) are automated. Judgment-required transitions (genuine buying intent, fit assessment) remain manual. The CRM must reflect reality at all times — Pipeline stage should be derivable from the Messages table.

---

## The Problem

The CRM didn't reflect reality. All 181 prospects were stuck at "New" despite 7 having been contacted. The Pipeline stage names ("Contacted," "In conversation," "Demo booked") were ambiguous and assumed a specific sales motion. There was no clear boundary between what should be automated and what requires human judgment.

---

## Options Considered

| # | Option | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| A | Patch the current gap only | Just fix the New → Contacted automation, leave naming and structure as-is |
| B | Design from first principles (chosen) | Redesign the entire lifecycle, rename stages for clarity, define automation boundaries, build only what's needed now |

---

## Why This Path

James explicitly asked for first-principles design rather than patching. The redesign produces a lifecycle that is verifiable (every stage derivable from Messages), universal (works across industries), and aligned with our Tier 1 / Tier 2 trust model. The naming changes are semantic, not structural — same number of stages, clearer meaning.

---

## Core Principles Applied

- **CRM reflects reality** — Every stage change is triggered by an observable event
- **System over person** — Automate the mechanical, protect the judgment
- **Earn the right** — Automation boundaries derived from Tier 1 / Tier 2 trust model
- **Simple systems that scale** — Same lifecycle works for 10 prospects or 10,000
- **Data over dogma** — Holding remaining automations until calibration data validates the design

---

## The Seven Stages

| Stage | Trigger | Auto? |
|---|---|---|
| New | Record created | Auto |
| Outreach Active | First email sent (→ `scripts/crm-update-send.py`) | **Auto** |
| Engaged | Inbound reply detected (→ inbox-pulse) | **Auto** (pending) |
| Qualified Opportunity | James confirms buying intent | Manual |
| Won | Subscription active | Auto + confirm |
| Campaign Complete | Final email + cooldown, no reply | **Auto** (pending) |
| Lost | Negative reply or James marks | Mixed |

## Risks Identified

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old stage names (Contacted, In conversation, Demo booked) cause confusion alongside new names | Low | Low | No data in old stages. Clean up after calibration confirms new names are right. |
| "Qualified Opportunity" is too high a bar — prospects get stuck at Engaged | Med | Med | Watch during calibration. If Engaged becomes a dumping ground, we may need an intermediate stage. |
| Automation creates false confidence — CRM says "Outreach Active" but email bounced | Low | Med | Script exits non-zero on API errors. Bounce detection via inbox-pulse should eventually trigger remediation. |

---

## Expected Outcome

- CRM immediately reflects outreach activity (no manual updates)
- Pipeline stage is auditable against Messages table
- Calibration data validates or refines the stage definitions
- Remaining automations (Engaged, Campaign Complete) are built on evidence, not theory
