# 07 — When To Break The Rules

> Principles are not dogma. They exist to make the right decision easier, not to replace judgment. This document defines the contexts where the standard outreach rules shift — and why.

---

## The General Rule

If you're considering breaking a rule, you must be able to articulate:
1. **Which rule** you're breaking
2. **Why the context is different** — specifically what makes this situation an exception
3. **What principle still applies** — you're not abandoning philosophy, you're applying it differently
4. **What you'll watch for** — how you'll know if the exception was wrong

If you can't articulate all four, don't break the rule.

---

## Exception 1: Warm Referrals

**Context:** The prospect has been introduced by someone they trust. "Sarah from ABC Painting said I should talk to you." The relationship doesn't start at zero — it starts with borrowed trust.

**Rules that shift:**
- **"Build a relationship before selling"** — The relationship has a head start. The introducer's trust transfers. The first email can be more direct.
- **"No product in first email"** — If the referrer has explained what we do, the prospect already has context. Re-explaining is redundant, not restrained.

**What stays the same:**
- Still lead with them, not us. "Sarah mentioned you've been growing fast — congratulations" not "Sarah said you need our product."
- Still earn the right to the next conversation. Borrowed trust is finite. Don't spend it all at once.

**Still wrong:** "Sarah said you need quote follow-up — here's our pricing." (Assumptive. Wastes borrowed trust.)

**Acceptable:** "Sarah mentioned you've been expanding the Adelaide team. She thought we should connect — she's seen what we've been working on with quote management. Happy to share more if you're curious."

---

## Exception 2: Existing Relationships

**Context:** You've already exchanged emails with this person. They know who you are. The cold outreach principles were designed for first contact.

**Rules that shift:**
- **"One question per email"** — In an ongoing conversation, multiple questions can be natural. "You mentioned the medical contracts are growing. Has that changed your quoting process? And are you still handling follow-up yourself?"
- **"No product mention"** — Once the conversation has established the gap, product context is appropriate. They've earned the right to know what you do.

**What stays the same:**
- Still lead with curiosity, not pitch.
- Still earn each escalation. Just because you've had two good emails doesn't mean you've earned a demo ask.
- Still sell certainty, not software.

---

## Exception 3: Painting / Trade Prospects (Travis Relevance)

**Context:** The prospect is in the same industry as our primary case study (Travis Krieg Painting). The case study is directly relevant because the business is similar.

**Rules that shift:**
- **"Travis as bridge, not opener"** — For painting prospects specifically, Travis can appear earlier. Not in the first sentence, but perhaps in the second email rather than the third.

**What stays the same:**
- Still don't lead with Travis in the first cold email. "You're a painter and we helped a painter" isn't insight — it's coincidence. It only becomes relevant after they've recognised their own gap.
- First email: still a question. "With a crew your size, how do you manage follow-up across multiple jobs?"
- Second email (after reply): "The reason I'm asking — we worked with Travis Krieg Painting here in Adelaide. Similar setup to yours — 5-person crew, residential and commercial. They recovered $177,555 in jobs they would have lost just by systematising their follow-up. That's why your question about tracking quotes caught my attention."

**The shift:** Travis moves from third-email to second-email for painting prospects. That's it. Not first email. Not opener.

---

## Exception 4: Second Emails (After a Reply)

**Context:** The prospect replied to the first email. They've engaged. The conversation has begun. The rules for cold outreach no longer fully apply.

**Rules that shift:**
- **Word count** — Can extend to 120-180 words. You've earned more attention.
- **Product context** — Light product context may be appropriate if the prospect's reply indicated curiosity about what we do. "The reason I asked about your quoting process — I work with a platform that helps service businesses track exactly this. Happy to share more if you're interested, but I was genuinely curious about how you handle it currently."
- **Multiple questions** — In a reply, asking a follow-up is natural. Don't interrogate, but you can go deeper.

**What stays the same:**
- Still don't pitch. Context is not a pitch. "I work with a platform" is context. "Our platform will solve all your problems" is a pitch.
- Still earn the right to the next ask. A reply to your first email earns a follow-up question. It doesn't earn a demo request.
- Still sell certainty, not software.

---

## Exception 5: The Prospect Asks Directly

**Context:** The prospect replies and explicitly asks what you do: "This sounds interesting — what exactly is it that you offer?"

**Rules that shift:**
- **"No product in first email"** — They asked. You answer. But answer honestly and briefly. Don't use the opening to pitch, even when invited.

**Acceptable:** "We built a platform that helps service businesses track what happens after they send a quote — so nothing falls through the cracks. The Adelaide painter I mentioned recovered $177,555 in jobs that would have otherwise been lost. Happy to walk you through how it works, but I'm also happy to just keep the conversation going here if you prefer."

**Still wrong:** "Great question! Not Forgotten Systems is the leading quote follow-up automation platform for Australian service businesses. Our AI-powered CRM..." (They asked. You don't need to pitch. Just answer.)

---

## What NEVER Changes (regardless of context)

These are the immutable principles. Context can shift tactics, not values.

1. **Never manipulate** — No false urgency, scarcity, or psychological tricks. Ever.
2. **Never lie** — About results, about customers, about capabilities.
3. **Never oversell** — Even when they're interested, restraint builds trust.
4. **Never make it about us** — The conversation is always about their business. We're here to help them, not impress them.
5. **Sell certainty, not software** — Even in a demo, the product is the absence of uncertainty.
6. **Earn every ask** — Context accelerates permission. It never replaces it.

---

## The Exception Test

Before deviating from any standard rule, ask:

> "Am I breaking this rule because the context genuinely demands it, or because breaking the rule is easier than following it?"

If it's the second, follow the rule.
