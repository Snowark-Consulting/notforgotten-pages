# Decision: Campaign Pulse Reporting Standard — Facts, Confidence, Risk, and CEO Decision

## The Decision

James Hardstaff established a permanent Campaign Pulse reporting standard with six structural requirements: (1) separate facts from interpretation using a five-part structure, (2) include confidence ratings for every interpretation, (3) add a Biggest Risk section, (4) add a Biggest Opportunity section, (5) challenge assumptions with counter-evidence, and (6) finish with one CEO Decision. The purpose of Campaign Pulse is to help James run the business — not to describe it.

---

## The Problem

The existing Campaign Pulse format (established in `growth-operations/SKILL.md` via the five-section table) was directionally correct but lacked specificity. It didn't distinguish between high-confidence observations and speculative hypotheses. It didn't proactively surface risk or opportunity. And it didn't end with a single, unambiguous recommendation for James. The report described the business; it didn't help run it.

James provided six refinements during the August 1 Campaign Pulse review to address these gaps permanently.

---

## The Six Refinements

### 1. Separate Facts from Interpretation

Every Campaign Pulse must follow this structure:

| Section | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **What happened** | Facts only. No interpretation. "7 sent, 0 replies at Day 3." |
| **What it means** | Analysis. Interpretation of the facts. |
| **Why we believe it happened** | Hypothesis. Causal reasoning. |
| **What we expect next** | Forecast. Prediction with reasoning. |
| **Recommended action** | Decision. What should be done. |

This keeps evidence clearly separated from opinion at every level.

### 2. Include Confidence for Every Interpretation

Facts don't need confidence ratings. Analysis, hypotheses, forecasts, and recommendations do.

| Rating | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | 90%+ | Well-supported by multiple lines of evidence, verified data, or confirmed by James |
| **Medium** | 70-89% | Reasonable inference from available data but single-source or limited sample |
| **Low** | <70% | Speculative — best judgment with significant uncertainty |

Confidence reflects evidence strength, not optimism. Format: "Confidence: High (90%)" after the interpretation.

### 3. Biggest Risk Section

Every Campaign Pulse must answer:

**"What is the single biggest risk to the business over the next seven days?"**

Possible answers include: nothing significant, client churn, low reply volume, technical risk, execution bottleneck. If there is genuinely no material risk, say so — but do the work to assess it first.

### 4. Biggest Opportunity Section

Every Campaign Pulse must answer:

**"What is the single biggest opportunity available right now?"**

This keeps focus on growth, not just operations. Even in quiet periods, there is always an opportunity — it may be preparing for the next wave, improving a process, or mining existing data for intelligence.

### 5. Challenge Assumptions

When writing "Why we believe it happened," Atlas must ask:

**"What evidence would prove me wrong?"**

If there is meaningful uncertainty, state it. Hypotheses must be clearly distinguished from conclusions. Correlation must be distinguished from causation. This prevents storytelling from masquerading as analysis.

### 6. Finish with One CEO Decision

Every Campaign Pulse must end with:

**"If James only does one thing because of this report, it should be..."**

One clear recommendation. No ambiguity. No menu of options. The single highest-leverage action.

## The Purpose

> The purpose of Campaign Pulse is not to describe the business.
> The purpose is to help me run the business.

Every report should leave James knowing:
- What changed
- Why it matters
- What he should do
- What can safely wait
