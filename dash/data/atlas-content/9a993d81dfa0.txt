# Decision: Relationship-First CRM Philosophy

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Decision** | The CRM's organising principle shifts from "opportunity management" to "relationship storytelling." Every customer is a relationship with chapters, not a collection of disconnected quote records. The governing question for every CRM change is: "Will this make it easier to understand this customer's history six months from now?" |
| **Problem** | An opportunity-centric CRM fragments customer relationships into disconnected records. Nicholas Zappacosta is one person with one relationship to QPaint — but an opportunity-centric view sees three separate quotes. The fragmented view hides the relationship narrative, makes it impossible to understand the customer journey at a glance, and creates work for James and clients who must reconstruct history from scattered records. |
| **Why** | Our product is trust. Trust is built over time through consistent, intelligent relationship management. If our own CRM cannot tell the complete story of a customer relationship, we cannot credibly help clients manage theirs. The CRM is not a database of quotes — it is the institutional memory of every customer interaction. |

## Context

This decision builds on the Relationship Continuation Protocol (`2026-07-29-relationship-continuation-protocol`) but goes further. The Continuation Protocol established that new scope from existing customers should update existing records rather than create duplicates. This decision establishes *why*: because the organising principle of the CRM is the customer relationship, not the individual quote.

The trigger was the Nicholas Zappacosta relationship at QPaint — three active threads (Logan Village State School, QAS Beenleigh, Port Adelaide Plaza) that are chapters in one evolving relationship with a government foreperson who keeps coming back to QPaint. Fragmenting this into three records would destroy the story.

## The Decision

### Mental Model: Relationship-First

**Before (opportunity-centric):**
```
QPaint Pipeline
├── Quote #1 — Logan Village ($145K)
├── Quote #2 — QAS Beenleigh (TBC)
└── Quote #3 — Port Adelaide Plaza (TBC)
```

**After (relationship-centric):**
```
Nicholas Zappacosta — Relationship with QPaint
├── First contact: 2026-07-28 — Email 1 introduction
├── Chapter 1: Logan Village State School ($145K) — engaged, awaiting confirmation
├── Chapter 2: QAS Beenleigh (TBC) — new scope, forwarded to Tas
├── Chapter 3: Port Adelaide Plaza (TBC) — new scope, forwarded to Tas
└── Current status: Multi-Opportunity Active — relationship strengthening
```

### The Governing Question

Before every CRM action, ask:

**"Does this create a clearer picture of the customer's journey?"**

- Yes → proceed
- No → stop and reassess
- Creates duplicates or fragments history → stop and find a better way

### The Test Question

Before every CRM action, ask:

**"Will this make it easier for James or the client to understand this customer's history six months from now?"**

- Yes → the action is correct
- No → the action needs to change
- Not sure → flag for review

### The Relationship Timeline

Every customer relationship must have a chronological timeline showing:

1. First contact (date and nature)
2. Every quote (date, value, status)
3. Every follow-up (date, channel, purpose)
4. Every reply (date, summary, classification)
5. Every project (date, scope, outcome)
6. Every variation (date, description, impact)
7. Every meeting or phone call (date, participants, outcome)
8. Every key decision (date, decision, rationale)
9. Current relationship status
10. Next recommended action

**Format:** Structured entries in `Outreach Notes`, with each entry following the pattern:

```
[YYYY-MM-DD] [Event Type] — [Description]. [Outcome/Next Step].
```

**Event types:**
- `First Contact` — Initial outreach
- `Email Sent` — Outbound communication
- `Reply Received` — Customer responded
- `Quote Sent` — New quotation provided
- `Scope Received` — New scope from customer
- `Call` — Phone conversation
- `Meeting` — In-person or video
- `Decision` — Customer made a choice
- `Status Change` — Relationship stage evolved
- `Handoff` — Escalated to client
- `Won` — Project awarded
- `Lost` — Project declined

### CRM Design Principles

1. **One record per customer relationship** — not one per quote
2. **Chronological narrative** — Outreach Notes tell the story in order
3. **Structured but human** — consistent format, natural language
4. **Glanceable** — James or client should understand the relationship in 30 seconds
5. **Six-month test** — every entry must make sense without context six months later

## Atlas's Challenge (Pre-Decision)

This is not a challenge to the direction — it is the right direction. The challenge is implementation:

1. **Retrospective timeline construction** — existing records have unstructured Outreach Notes. Building timelines from fragmented history requires judgment.
2. **Airtable limitations** — Outreach Notes is a single multilineText field. A truly structured timeline would benefit from a linked Timeline Events table with typed entries. This is worth considering as we scale.
3. **Consistency across clients** — QPaint is the first client. The relationship-first philosophy must be embedded in every future client pipeline from day one, not retrofitted.

James's response: The philosophy is correct. Start with structured Outreach Notes. Consider a Timeline Events table when the evidence supports it.

## Expected Outcomes

1. Every customer relationship tells a complete, chronological story
2. James or Tas can understand any customer's history in 30 seconds
3. No duplicate records fragment customer relationships
4. The CRM becomes the institutional memory of every customer interaction
5. The six-month test is passed by every CRM entry

- Future client pipelines adopt this philosophy from day one
