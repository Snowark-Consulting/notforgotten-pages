What This Analysis Teaches Us About Not Forgotten

1. The sequence is genuinely a safety net — not the brain. The Email 2 Next Touchpoint date (Aug 3) told us to look at these 32 customers. That was useful. It did its job. But what we found — 32 silent customers with 32 different contexts — demonstrates why the sequence should trigger analysis, not action.

2. Not Forgotten should optimise for "appropriate next action taken" — not "number of follow-ups sent." In this analysis of 32 customers, only 3 warrant action now. 17 should be left alone. 4 need client input. 8 need investigation. If we had sent Email 3 to all 32, we would have contacted 29 people who didn't need contacting — including government procurement officers, strata managers, and standard residential customers who are simply still deciding. The metric that matters is not "did we follow up?" — it's "did we take the right action for this specific customer?"

3. Different customer types need different decision journeys — not different nurture emails. The previous client who hasn't replied needs a phone call from Tas. The government procurement officer needs patience. The strata manager needs to not be hassled. The high-intent lead at 57 days needs an honest question. The Facebook lead at 14 days needs space. These are not different email templates. They are different commercial strategies.

4. Two emails without reply is a signal that the third contact should be different — not just delayed. Across 32 customers, zero replied to Email 1 or Email 2. That doesn't mean the emails failed — it means the next contact needs to be materially different: a different channel, a different question, a different tempo, or no contact at all.

5. Client input is a legitimate step in the recovery workflow. We cannot determine the right action for Richard Zhang ($145K, builder referral) without knowing whether Tas knows the builder. We cannot determine it for Helen Walsh ($110K, 81d body corp) without knowing whether the quote is still valid. "Ask QPaint" is not a failure of the system — it is the system recognising the limits of its own knowledge.

6. The product becomes more valuable when it makes fewer automated decisions and more intelligent recommendations. A system that sends Email 3 to everyone because the calendar says so is an automation platform. A system that analyses 32 customers and says "3 need action, 17 should wait, 4 need client input, 8 need investigation" is a commercial intelligence platform. The second one is worth paying for.

7. Founder Approval Mode is not a bottleneck — it is the mechanism that prevents the system from acting on incomplete information. This analysis would not exist without it. The Email 3 template would have been sent. The government officers would have received a third email. The previous clients would have received another template instead of a phone call. Founder Approval Mode created the pause that made this analysis possible.
