Case study

How one Adelaide painter recovered $180,115 from quotes he'd already sent

14 jobs. Real money that was already sitting in his business. All it took was following up.

You send a quote. The customer says they'll think about it. A week goes by. Then two. Then a month.

You mean to follow up, but you're on the tools all day. The phone rings during a cut-in and goes to voicemail. By the time you think about that quote again, the moment has passed. So it sits there. Unanswered. Forgotten.

Every one of those quiet quotes is a job you might have won. For most trades businesses, they add up to tens of thousands of dollars a year, just sitting there.

Travis Krieg Painting had exactly this problem. Here's what happened when he started following up properly.

Too busy doing the work to chase the work

Travis runs a five-person painting business. Everyone is busy. Quotes went out and, too often, disappeared. Customers would say "we've been meaning to call you." Jobs went to whoever answered the phone first.

There was no process. No reminders. No way to know which quotes were still alive and which were dead. Follow-up was the thing that always slipped to tomorrow.

He didn't hire a salesperson, and he didn't buy expensive software. He simply started following up on every quote, consistently, in the right order, at the right time.
